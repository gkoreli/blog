# Prose and messages

Use for a wording edit, documentation, PR descriptions, release notes, article polish or casual copy. Preserve code, commands, identifiers, quotations and precise technical terms.

## Clear prose

Prefer familiar, literal words and active voice when the actor is known. Remove repetition, generic preambles and flourish that add no meaning. Keep a metaphor or longer term when a literal substitute loses precision. Vary sentence length naturally; do not turn a style preference into a mechanical rewrite.

Keep the qualifications that change a claim. State real uncertainty plainly without repeating it in every sentence. Represent competing views fairly, avoid straw men and avoid repeated “not X, but Y” constructions; normally use at most one per piece. Use only the examples the reader needs.

State the point rather than announcing the section or explaining why a title, joke or line works. Avoid neighboring paragraph endings that each try to be a punchline. Keep humor only when it contributes. Read substantial finished prose aloud or perform an equivalent rhythm pass.

Preserve meaningful fragments, repetition, capitalization, exclamation and self-interruption. Do not manufacture them to imitate a human. In an exposed essay, identify the problem and its effect for the author instead of supplying a replacement sentence.

Compare the polished version with its source for changes in meaning, emphasis, certainty and voice. Restore any consequential distinction lost in smoothing. A rewritten passage must not remain presented as an exact quotation.

## Goga’s informal voice

For Reddit, X, casual replies and outreach written as Goga:

- Lead with the useful result or practical point; explain afterward. A research-process preamble, greeting, article announcement or biography should not delay the value.
- Follow the supplied reasoning as well as its punctuation: a concrete observation can grow into a tentative explanation, another possibility and a useful next check. Preserve those connections instead of turning every thought into a standalone slogan or a confident verdict.
- Use ordinary language and the natural rhythm of the supplied voice. Lowercase openings, comma-led thoughts and unfinished sentences are welcome when they fit; do not regularize them into corporate copy. Follow explicit owner preferences for casual roughness without making typos or slang a substitute for the thinking.
- Keep relevant firsthand context. Do not remove a useful account of the actual work to make the reply generic.
- State strong supplied opinions as opinions. Keep the conditions that change a result’s meaning.
- Do not append a question, invitation, pitch or follow request automatically. Add one only when wanted and useful.

## Documentation, PRs and release notes

Lead with the concrete change, resulting behavior and why it matters to the intended reader. Scale explanation to the change; a small fix does not require a full editorial workflow. Preserve technical identifiers and state validation and material limits accurately. Describe the final implementation for someone who has not seen the conversation; omit abandoned conversational history unless it explains a real tradeoff.
