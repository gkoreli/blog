# Editorial Workflow

1. Identify the article's central question, claim, or experience with [shape-article](../SKILL.md).
2. For substantive practical articles, apply [earned-trust-writing](../../earned-trust-writing/SKILL.md). Use supplied experience and documented work; ask for missing substance.
3. Apply the governing form: [blog-writing](../../blog-writing/SKILL.md) for engineering, [personal-essays](../../personal-essays/SKILL.md) for personal writing, or [oss-radar](../../oss-radar/SKILL.md) for project research. The agent edits but does not draft exposed essays.
4. Revise with the author's feedback. Preserve unresolved questions and material contradictions rather than inventing a conclusion.
5. Verify dates, attributions, quotations, external links, repositories, and technical claims against their sources. Open or search each external source. Match consequential claims to the conditions and evidence supporting them.
6. After the substance is stable, use [article-discovery-positioning](../../article-discovery-positioning/SKILL.md) for titles, metadata, and links. Use [shareable-engineering](../../shareable-engineering/SKILL.md) for an engineering pre-publication review.
7. Apply [polish-prose](../../polish-prose/SKILL.md) last. In exposed essays, flag problems for the author instead of supplying replacement prose.

## Prompt provenance

AI-assisted collaborative posts include complete human prompts, in chronological order, when they materially shape the article, research, claims, metadata, provenance, or publication decision. Exposed essays and OSS Radar issues omit prompt files under their respective authorship models.

Exclude later repository housekeeping that changes none of the published artifacts. Follow [owner quotations](../../blog-writing/references/owner-quotations.md) when attributing words to Goga, and [footprint accounting](../../blog-writing/references/research-footprint.md) when publishing measured research usage.
