# Openings and Section Structure

## Opening and Section Discipline

For explanatory engineering articles and OSS Radar:

1. Open with the supported result, problem, or decision that gives the reader a reason to continue. State what happened and why it matters in ordinary language.
2. Follow with a short list of the main findings or claims the body will explain.
3. Introduce background and technical detail as needed to explain those findings. Organize for understanding rather than following the chronology of the work.
4. Each explanatory section begins with its point and ends with its takeaway or remaining question. Reading those paragraphs together should convey the argument without the implementation detail.
5. Keep the opening accessible. When moving into instructions for implementers, identify that audience in one sentence.

For an exposed essay, the opening may be a scene, conflict, or unresolved experience. Do not impose a summary, bullet list, technical section, or lesson on that form. Its first paragraph must still give the reader a reason to continue.

Headlines and openings emphasize the main reader problem or result. Include an implementation constraint there only when it defines that problem or materially limits the result.

## Open With a Result That Creates Questions

For an evidence-led article, compare the strongest results across its research and experiments before choosing the opening. Select by consequence, relevance, comprehensibility, and evidence quality. Neither the largest percentage nor the author's own experiment automatically takes priority.

- Name the actor or system, observed action, and consequence before introducing an unfamiliar metric.
- Keep the population, comparator, and essential condition beside a number. Distinguish percentage changes, percentage-point changes, and composite scores.
- Attribute external findings and link their evidence beside the claim. Explain exclusions and denominators before they can mislead.
- Explain the experiment, mechanism, implication, and possible response in the body. State what remains unknown.
- Use numbers only when they clarify a consequential supported result. Do not imply surprise, success, or generality the evidence does not establish.
