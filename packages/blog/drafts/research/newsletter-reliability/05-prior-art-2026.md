# Newsletter signup protection: original rationale and 2026 prior art

Checked September 8 PDT / September 9 UTC, 2026. TASK-0127 remains in progress. This pass reconstructs the recorded decision and compares primary guidance and implementations; it does not measure comparative signup completion or abuse prevention.

Later owner direction: keep this personal blog simple while allowing ownership of the platform. The [current scope](03-protection-options.md#current-scope-simple-signup-on-our-existing-platform) governs the work. A managed service remains an option; the agent's recommendation was not an owner-selected migration. The implementation practices and comparison below are retained research, not a requirement to build new infrastructure or every candidate.

The original choice was a fast, inexpensive way to add spam protection to an urgent newsletter launch on the existing Cloudflare stack. The stronger prior art supports controls at several stages, including before confirmation mail. It does not establish a universally better CAPTCHA. The comparison must also include delegating subscription operations to a maintained newsletter service.

## Why Turnstile was added

The [first ADR-0010](https://github.com/gkoreli/blog/blob/2b3863c2a9bb3417d9c17e48fae39aeb53a9fd3d/docs/adr/0010-email-subscriptions.md) records a post receiving substantial Reddit attention while the blog had no way to retain readers through email. Its constraints were to ship during that traffic window, reuse Workers and D1, and stay within free services. It selected Turnstile for spam protection and initially chose Managed mode. This is the repository's account of the decision, not a newly verified traffic measurement or a verbatim owner directive.

| Recorded step | What the evidence establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Initial implementation, April 8 UTC, `2b3863c` | Turnstile preceded pending storage and confirmation mail; the ADR initially deferred an additional rate limiter | A measured attack on this newsletter, or a comparison proving the challenge sufficient |
| [Hardening, `e83ce6c`](https://github.com/gkoreli/blog/commit/e83ce6c7eacb2eef065c22834907f985ad3feb83) | Added a native rate limiter and recorded Workers examples as inspiration | Those projects' production reliability, or an enforceable global sending ceiling |
| [Invisible-mode change, April 8, `568a959`](https://github.com/gkoreli/blog/commit/568a95988249e4ca7198f0f7235c314eb639b5ef) | The ADR explicitly favored removing the visible widget from the minimalist design | Equivalent effectiveness across modes, successful script loading, or zero reader friction |
| Initialization fixes, `185e6f2` and `60f75db`; April 11 verifier fixes, `b3671eb` and `da2c44c` | Integration work continued after launch | Continuous failure since April or additional identified victims |

The original ADR also asserted stronger protection and a trust benefit from the visible badge without a cited comparison. Those assertions are historical assumptions, not evidence to retain Turnstile now. The later implementation and incident supply actual maintenance costs to consider. They still do not measure Turnstile's false-positive rate.

The inspected launch ADRs and commit history contain no systematic comparison with a maintained hosted signup flow, a self-hosted proof-of-work integration, or a tested flow without a challenge. That bounds this finding to the preserved record; it cannot establish what the author considered elsewhere. The existing [ADR](../../../../../docs/adr/0010-email-subscriptions.md) now links this reassessment.

## The abuse problem exists before confirmation

The Swiss NCSC reported increased subscription-bombing reports on February 10, 2026: legitimate websites were made to send unwanted registration confirmations. Its account explains why authenticated mail can still participate in an attack. This is current threat evidence, not evidence that gkoreli.com experienced such abuse. [NCSC report](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26w6-en).

A [2026 author-hosted study by Mathias Payer and Candid Wüest](https://hexhive.epfl.ch/publications/files/26CACM.pdf) analyzes 24 campaigns containing 46,970 unwanted emails and recommends both confirmation and pre-send anti-automation measures. Its campaign sample is useful evidence of the mechanism; it is not a controlled comparison of Turnstile, ALTCHA, or our proposed limits. The PDF has placeholder journal metadata, and the publisher/DOI pages were not retrievable in this pass; no independently verified publication date or peer-review status is asserted here.

This was known before 2026. [Spamhaus's September 2016 incident account](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/threat-intelligence/subscription-bombing-coi-captcha-and-the-next-generation-of-mail-bombs/) explains that confirmation messages themselves sustained an attack. [M3AAWG's October 2017 recommendation](https://www.m3aawg.org/WebFormAttacks) recommends protecting public forms and identifying form-triggered email. Its linked [mail-header proposal](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-levine-mailbomb-header/) is currently an expired individual Internet-Draft, not an adopted RFC or a 2026 requirement.

Per-address cooldowns bound our contribution to one recipient's flood. A total send ceiling bounds our volume and cost. Neither prevents thousands of separate sites from sending that recipient one email each. Likewise, a low-volume attack can remain below our local trigger. These are residual risks that a no-challenge decision must explicitly accept; low volume alone is not evidence that pre-send protection is unnecessary.

## Prior art that changes the comparison

All provider behavior below is documented behavior checked during this pass, unless a pinned implementation or firsthand issue is specified. No candidate was deployed or exercised with real recipients.

| Prior art | Relevant mechanism | Implication and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Mailchimp: fake signups](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-fake-signups/) | Hosted-form reCAPTCHA, honeypots, and throttling when one address is added across multiple audiences | A provider can observe cross-customer patterns our single blog cannot. The page's absolute claim that bots cannot pass its checkbox is not adopted as an effectiveness result |
| [Kit CAPTCHA FAQ](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2502481-captcha-faq), dated April 30, 2026 | CAPTCHA cannot be disabled on its forms; visible challenges are conditional, including repeated IP/address activity. The FAQ lists compatibility problems with some integrations | Conditional presentation is not absence of a required protection mechanism. Incognito can trigger a challenge; no measured completion rate is supplied |
| [Buttondown firewall](https://docs.buttondown.com/firewall) and [April 19, 2026 changes](https://buttondown.com/blog/firewall-improvements) | Configurable filtering, conditional hosted challenges, automatic attack mode, operator notification, and test addresses. Flagged submissions can be rejected or retained as Blocked for review | A concrete operational model to compare against our custom flow. Custom hosting domains cannot show the CAPTCHA fallback: flagged subscribers are blocked. This restriction matters to our branded-site evaluation |
| [listmonk v6.2.0](https://github.com/knadh/listmonk/releases/tag/v6.2.0), released June 26, 2026 | Its [pinned public form](https://github.com/knadh/listmonk/blob/ef0a75872463f10a4848af6c547d1c057405453a/cmd/public.go) has a honeypot and configurable CAPTCHA; its [verification module](https://github.com/knadh/listmonk/blob/ef0a75872463f10a4848af6c547d1c057405453a/internal/captcha/captcha.go) implements ALTCHA and hCaptcha, challenge expiry, and replay checks | Useful source-level prior art. This was inspection of two modules, not a full routing/security audit or production test. The Go/Postgres application is not a drop-in Worker replacement |
| [ALTCHA server integration](https://altcha.org/docs/integration/server/) and [security recommendations](https://altcha.org/docs/integration/security-recommendations/) | Custom integrations can generate challenges and verify cryptographically on their own server; they must enforce expiry, single use, and rate limits | Removes an external Siteverify call in that design, while leaving browser computation, JavaScript, state, and maintenance requirements. Proof of work imposes cost; it does not authenticate a person |
| [Resend idempotency keys](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys) and [email events](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/event-types) | Deduplicates retries of the same send for 24 hours; distinguishes API acceptance, recipient-server delivery, delay, failure, bounce, and complaint | Directly reusable in our existing stack. Deduplication does not bound distinct sends, and delivery to a mail server is not inbox placement or a human reading the email |

ALTCHA deserves a test, not an automatic replacement verdict. A [July 28, 2026 listmonk operator report](https://github.com/knadh/listmonk/issues/3170) describes unwanted confirmations despite ALTCHA and double opt-in. It is a firsthand report, with no independently reproduced efficacy comparison. ALTCHA also documents a [July 30 library verification fix](https://github.com/altcha-org/altcha-lib/security/advisories/GHSA-rx38-p542-v527); the vendor's [advisory listing](https://altcha.org/pt/security-advisory/) identifies affected language implementations and patched versions. A prototype must pin a corrected library and test the actual verification mode. This is not a claim that every ALTCHA integration or listmonk's Go path has that defect.

The original ADR's smaller Workers projects remain inspiration, with narrower evidence. [SamirPaulb's current README](https://github.com/SamirPaulb/newsletter-and-contact-system) advertises Turnstile, native limits, and KV/D1 storage; [LetterDrop's repository](https://github.com/i365dev/LetterDrop) is available. Their existence does not validate a production reliability claim. Their code and deployed services were not fully audited here.

## Practices to adopt or test in this stack

The following is an engineering synthesis from the sources and our [application audit](02-client-audit.md), not a universal 2026 checklist or a claim that these repairs are implemented.

1. **Control both send routes.** Apply independent address cooldowns, aggregate send reservations, and request limits to signup and resend. A successful challenge must not override suppression or send ceilings. Cloudflare's [native rate limiter](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/rate-limit/) is location-local and eventually consistent; it cannot provide exact global accounting by itself.
2. **Make sending recoverable.** Persist an admitted pending operation and durable send intent before claiming acceptance; use a stable operation key for provider retries, retain the returned message ID, and record later outcomes. Our [confirmation sender](https://github.com/gkoreli/blog/blob/c4c924fa9005a1e30e99862ab5e871d8fa6b257c/packages/newsletter/src/email.ts) currently omits an idempotency key and discards the successful response body. A queue or outbox is a design candidate, not a requirement for a particular new product. Bound storage, retries, and work under dependency failures.
3. **Keep confirmation and suppression correct.** Activate only through the intended confirmation transition, preserve unsubscribe/bounce state, and test repeated submissions and automated link fetching. [Gmail's current sender guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/15263077?hl=en) calls for address confirmation and easy unsubscribe. A confirmation link fetch is an observed event, not proof of a distinct human or the original submitter's identity.
4. **Define verifier failure behavior.** Missing/bad configuration, token rejection, and network/provider outage need distinct internal outcomes and usable reader messages. An outage fallback that sends mail must remain inside the same enforceable budgets. An operator alert should identify configuration failure. Do not silently activate an address or promise mail delivery on a failed operation.
5. **Test access as well as rejection.** Exercise Safari, privacy settings, script failure, slow networks, keyboard operation, and assistive technology where available. Keep a usable recovery route for readers who cannot complete verification. Treat a hidden field or an address heuristic as a signal with possible false positives, not as proof of abuse.
6. **Measure the stages without logging the form.** Correlate submission, verifier decision, pending state, send outcome, and activation using opaque operation IDs. Keep addresses in restricted subscription state when required, with bounded retention; keep them out of ordinary diagnostics. Consider a bounded review state for admitted but blocked submissions, separate from active subscribers. This would support future investigation without treating an unverified address as consent. No-challenge intake still needs admission and storage limits. Neither every rejected address nor every browser event needs permanent storage.

[OWASP's bot-management guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Bot_Management_and_Anti-Automation_Cheat_Sheet.html) supports endpoint-specific, layered controls, monitored decisions, and graduated responses. Its provider examples must still be checked: it groups Turnstile under numerical risk scoring, whereas the documented [Siteverify response](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/) exposes a success boolean and verification metadata, not the application-configurable bot score that wording implies. We should not design thresholds around a field our API does not return.

## Earlier comparison protocol — superseded as the next work

After this review, Goga narrowed the requirement to a simple capability suitable for a personal blog, then questioned the assumption that this required outsourcing. The [current existing-platform scope](03-protection-options.md#current-scope-simple-signup-on-our-existing-platform) takes precedence over the comparison below. Preserve this protocol as research; do not build each candidate or add custom send infrastructure without a concrete remaining need.

The earlier conversational preference for evaluating a widget-free flow first was an untested engineering hypothesis. This review qualifies it: protecting the first send and reducing maintenance both deserve more weight. The immediate comparison should be a repaired custom flow versus a maintained provider's supported signup flow. A self-hosted challenge and a no-challenge design remain candidates under explicit constraints. Enforcement policy, verifier choice, and ownership are separate decisions; the following tracks are not mutually exclusive product categories.

| Candidate | Evidence needed before choosing it |
|---|---|
| Repaired mandatory Turnstile plus shared send controls | Correct credentials; full signup/confirmation; bounded recovery from each dependency failure; recorded browser friction and maintenance burden |
| Conditional Turnstile plus shared send controls | A server-enforced trigger and transition policy, successful step-up/retry behavior, and explicit treatment of abuse below the trigger |
| No challenge plus shared send controls | Enforced concurrent/distributed send and storage limits, legitimate completion, and acceptance of the remaining first-message abuse within those limits |
| Self-hosted ALTCHA plus shared send controls | Corrected pinned verification library, replay/expiry/concurrency tests, slow-device measurements, and distributed state design appropriate to Workers |
| Maintained hosted signup and subscriber management | Verify the exact hosted/embedded/custom-domain path, confirmation, export and suppression behavior, operational visibility, current cost, and reader recovery. Do not assume an arbitrary API integration inherits hosted-form protection |

Before any comparative rollout, record the actual per-address cooldown, total send and storage ceilings, retry limit, browser timeout, observation window, and operator notification path. These values remain unset; they depend on the permitted sending budget and acceptable reader wait. A budget can itself be exhausted to deny legitimate subscriptions, so test the recovery and escalation policy too.

Use the same local cases for each candidate: new/pending/active/unsubscribed/bounced states; repeated and concurrent requests; script failure; expired or reused challenges; bad configuration; verifier outage; database/limiter failure; provider rejection and ambiguous timeout. Expected invariants include no activation from an invalid token, no repeated provider send from a retry of the same operation, no send past a reserved ceiling, and no success message that contradicts the recorded stage. Test key/limit transitions so a deployment cannot silently remove protection.

Only then perform the bounded real-browser and designated-recipient acceptance checks. For a monitored trial, retain an exact window and counts by outcome; stop on a budget violation, lost durable intent, or incorrect subscription transition. A challenge rejection is not independently labeled abuse, and a controlled success is not a population success rate. With a small sample, make a reversible decision from these guarantees and disclosed residual risks rather than claiming statistical superiority.

Next action: complete TASK-0124's configuration/error repair and specify shared send controls in TASK-0126; compare the supported hosted flow before spending time building a new verifier. TASK-0127 is complete only when the chosen design, tested evidence, accepted tradeoffs, rollback, and conditions for reversal are recorded. This research did not change credentials, production protection, subscriber data, billing, or sending.

## Research boundary

Sources include launch Git history, current primary provider guidance, one pinned listmonk release and two modules, an author-hosted study, and a firsthand issue report. Searches also surfaced Ghost community reports and general advice pages; neither was treated as an audited implementation or comparative evidence. The initial listmonk documentation URL failed, and older ALTCHA documentation URLs failed or returned no text; current ALTCHA integration pages and pinned listmonk source supplied the relevant evidence. No published claim here depends on those failed retrievals.

No real signup experiment, provider purchase, inbox test, production SQL, abuse simulation against a live service, or comparative effectiveness benchmark ran in this pass. The unpublished [article](article.md) now includes the original rationale and the widened decision; its governing question remains unresolved.
