## Cohort theory and limits

Six of twelve explicitly address coding-agent behavior/workflows: Modern Web Guidance, Stroq, tmux-restore, Waymark, STE80 and Kairos. Maintained knowledge, host policy translation, recoverable state and artifact decoding are concrete continuing responsibilities. The reader decision is how much a tool takes on and what work remains.

The hypothesis that this work becomes more valuable as output gets cheaper is editorial, not measured demand or a maintainer roadmap. A sound rival explanation is the HN audience plus the sample's attention threshold and direct-GitHub rule. The market interpretation weakens if existing agents absorb these capabilities reliably or extra operations cost more attention than they save. The manuscript centers the supported responsibility comparison rather than presenting the sample as industry growth.

No pricing comparison, security certification, adoption count, broad maintenance prediction or legal advice follows. Open/closed issue states were checked when they change a claim, but closure alone is never proof of fixed behavior. Before publication, recheck consequential live issues and model terms; do not silently replace the recorded pins.
