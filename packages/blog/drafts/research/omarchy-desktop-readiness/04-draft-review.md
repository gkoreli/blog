# Draft scope, evidence and review

November 14, 2026, America/Los_Angeles.

## Result and editorial choice

The [first manuscript](article.md) explains why Omarchy is attracting attention and what evidence a personal Mac-switching decision still needs. It is a sourced inquiry with a proposed experiment sequence. It is not a firsthand Omarchy review or a completed migration verdict.

Title: **Omarchy Is Taking Off. Should I Leave My Mac?** The first sentence names the product proposition; the opening explicitly discloses no firsthand use and connects the existing Linux measurement to the wider decision within the first fifty words.

The reader problem is whether enthusiasm and narrowly measured speed justify changing an entire computing setup. The benchmark, the multiplier calculation, the source-level extension answer and the experiment table deliver that promise. Plausible discovery routes are the existing worktree article, searches about switching from Mac to Omarchy, and interest in Omarchy's customization interfaces. These are editorial hypotheses; no query-demand or referral analysis was performed here.

The earlier working title, “Linux Won My Benchmark. Would Omarchy Win My Workday?”, tied the piece to the owned experiment but left the product and wider personal decision less immediate. The chosen title keeps both visible without claiming an adoption outcome.

Suggested publication metadata, deferred until publication is authorized:

- `title` and H1: `Omarchy Is Taking Off. Should I Leave My Mac?`
- `seoTitle`: `Should I Leave My Mac for Omarchy? Evidence and Experiments`
- `description`: `Why Omarchy's defaults, agents and hardware work attract developers, and what I would measure before replacing my Mac. I haven't used Omarchy.`
- Section: engineering. Candidate tags: omarchy, linux, macos, agents.
- No publication date, live slug or canonical URL has been assigned.
