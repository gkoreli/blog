## Code facts beyond the READMEs

**C1 — ALCE adds references without proving them.** Its [post-hoc citation script](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE/blob/246c476a4edfc564266b7346b6e29ef4861ae937/post_hoc_cite.py#L38-L70) finds the best matching document for an uncited sentence and inserts its index. There is no entailment acceptance test in this insertion path. That makes it a useful example of why citation creation and support assessment are separate operations.

**C2 — ALCE evaluates the relationship.** In [the evaluator](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE/blob/246c476a4edfc564266b7346b6e29ef4861ae937/eval.py#L340-L429), missing or out-of-range references fail the sentence's support test. Otherwise an NLI model checks the cited passages jointly. For multiple citations, additional checks assess whether each supports the claim or contributes necessary evidence. The returned precision and recall are means of per-answer scores. These are judgments of textual support, not measurements of causal reliance or proof that a source is true.

**C3 — Self-RAG exposes a scoring policy.** The [long-form decoding implementation](https://github.com/AkariAsai/self-rag/blob/1fcdc420e48f50a7d7ab1ece5494221b93252e99/retrieval_lm/run_long_form_static.py#L72-L95) combines relevance, support, and utility scores derived from token probabilities; partial support contributes half the fully supported weight. Its [retrieval branch](https://github.com/AkariAsai/self-rag/blob/1fcdc420e48f50a7d7ab1ece5494221b93252e99/retrieval_lm/run_long_form_static.py#L148-L238) exposes always/adaptive/no-retrieval modes. This static long-form script passes supplied `ctxs` back into segment generation; do not describe it as fresh web search after every sentence.

**C4 — STORM's questions change its evidence pool.** [Knowledge curation](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/fb951af7744dab086e34962e9bc6fe878e145f83/knowledge_storm/storm_wiki/modules/knowledge_curation.py#L204-L243) converts a question into bounded search queries, retrieves results, and retains the queries, results, and synthesized answer. It can exclude the target Wikipedia URL to prevent evaluation leakage. [Section writing](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/fb951af7744dab086e34962e9bc6fe878e145f83/knowledge_storm/storm_wiki/modules/article_generation.py#L144-L174) passes numbered snippets to the model, with a 1,500-word information limit. A collected page is not necessarily present in the section's model input.

**C5 — STORM reconciles citations across sections.** [Reference merging](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/fb951af7744dab086e34962e9bc6fe878e145f83/knowledge_storm/storm_wiki/modules/storm_dataclass.py#L188-L207) assigns a unified index per URL, combines snippets for repeated URLs, and maps section-local indexes into that reference list. This is useful provenance plumbing. It does not itself judge the accuracy of the cited passage.

**C6 — Co-STORM's moderator has a specific input.** Its [grounded-question module](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/fb951af7744dab086e34962e9bc6fe878e145f83/knowledge_storm/collaborative_storm/modules/grounded_question_generation.py#L81-L115) receives unused snippets, formats them with indexes, combines them with the knowledge-base summary and last utterance, and returns the next question with cited information. The interesting design is what this role sees and does; multiple model identities alone explain little.

**C7 — OpenScholar implements a bounded revision pipeline.** Its [runner](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar/blob/0e9b8fb912273d3dae39e593da86e4f6d3bf8de1/src/open_scholar.py#L606-L708) saves the initial result, processes at most three feedback items, optionally adds Semantic Scholar results, and runs post-hoc attribution when enabled. The peS2o retrieval call inside the feedback branch is commented out; additional retrieval there depends on `ss_retriever`. Its [attribution function](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar/blob/0e9b8fb912273d3dae39e593da86e4f6d3bf8de1/src/open_scholar.py#L527-L604) asks the generator to revise citation-bearing text. This is a model-mediated check, not an external fact guarantee. The same file's [optional ranking adjustment](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar/blob/0e9b8fb912273d3dae39e593da86e4f6d3bf8de1/src/open_scholar.py#L40-L60) adds normalized paper citation counts when `norm_cite` is enabled: popularity can be an explicit retrieval preference, distinct from claim support.

### M1 — What OpenScholar's citation score establishes

The [Nature supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-10072-4/MediaObjects/41586_2025_10072_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), §2.3.3 (PDF p5 / printed p4), specifies `osunlp/attrscore-flan-t5-xl`, follows ALCE's citation precision/recall formulation, and skips sentences shorter than 50 characters. Its Scholar-CS rubric mixes general answer requirements with expert-identified content ingredients (§2.3.1); this is not a simple fraction of factually correct sentences.

The paper-linked [ScholarQABench evaluator](https://github.com/AkariAsai/ScholarQABench/blob/95e6fc52b0a8a0ce0a74956029991e3bb00c38b9/scripts/citation_correctness_eval.py#L355-L475), `main` baseline `95e6fc52b0a8a0ce0a74956029991e3bb00c38b9` (Aug 13, 2025), computes per-answer precision and recall and macro-averages each. It also carries the previous citation set forward when a subsequent eligible sentence has no explicit marker. [The command's output path](https://github.com/AkariAsai/ScholarQABench/blob/95e6fc52b0a8a0ce0a74956029991e3bb00c38b9/scripts/citation_correctness_eval.py#L741-L758) exports P/R, not the final citation F1. The final aggregation was not found in the inspected paper, supplement, or released evaluator. **Do not assert whether the published F1 is the mean of per-answer F1 scores or the harmonic mean of macro P/R.** Report it as the paper's citation F1 score, with this reproducibility limit in the research record.

### Comparison traps to avoid

- **“STORM proved equal verifiability.”** No: the study did not detect a difference. Its identical threshold-rating shares do not prove equivalence.
- **“OpenScholar made 39.5% of citations correct.”** No: that number is the reported combined F1 score, with model-based assessment and an unconfirmed final aggregation step.
- **“The same model searched the same documents.”** The OpenScholar base-model comparison retains a common OSDS source, but changes reranking, feedback, attribution, and potentially the evidence supplied.
- **“Self-RAG proves adaptive web research wins.”** Its cited ASQA setup uses pre-retrieved evidence; its small ablation excludes the adaptive comparison.
- **“The latest systems fail at the old rate.”** None of these historical results estimates today's general assistants, their organic use of this blog, or publisher referral traffic.

## Competing theories and editorial selection

| Theory | Whose view | Evidence for | Evidence against | Direction state | What would disprove it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Better question selection is a valuable research product | STORM/Co-STORM maintainers | C4–C6 and P3–P4 | Organization and exploration can improve without demonstrated source-validity gains | Implemented; outcomes reported | A matched-budget, matched-model comparison finds no useful exploration or writing benefit |
| Source support improves when retrieval and revision are explicit parts of generation | OpenScholar maintainers; Self-RAG supplies earlier related work | C3/C7, P2/P5 | Multiple components change; model judges remain fallible; some competitors score better on citation metrics | Implemented; outcomes reported | Component ablations or repeated external evaluations remove the claimed advantage |
| The decisive gain may be evidence selection and explicit assessment, rather than agent count | Editorial inference | ALCE's simpler baseline can beat inline search; Co-STORM's moderator ablation; OpenScholar's reranking ablation | Findings span unlike tasks and cannot be pooled | Inference, not a general measured law | Matched-budget comparisons isolate a benefit from added agent roles despite identical evidence and assessment |

**STORM/Co-STORM: research is also deciding what to ask.** Feature it for builders who want to inspect and steer how a report's evidence is assembled. Its source mapping, conversation record, and moderator input give the product substance beyond polished prose. The hard question is whether the chosen perspectives reveal important omissions or repeatedly elaborate an attractive but incomplete framing. The operator still supplies models, retrieval services or documents, and editorial judgment. The maintainers themselves describe the [system as research assistance rather than publication-ready writing](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/fb951af7744dab086e34962e9bc6fe878e145f83/README.md#overview-try-storm-now).

**OpenScholar: the strongest section on engineering source support.** Feature it for builders of scientific search and synthesis systems. It exposes a corpus, retrieval components, a generation/revision process, and an associated evaluation suite. The reader can examine which part improved an answer and which claim remains unsupported. The hard question is how faithfully a local deployment matches the tested corpus, configuration, and evaluator. It is research infrastructure with operating work, not a promise that a scientific-looking reference settles a scientific question.

Across this four-project sample, references appear at different points: post-hoc attachment in ALCE, segment scoring in Self-RAG, research organization in STORM/Co-STORM, and revision in OpenScholar. That is the useful pattern to carry into the article. A publisher's request log observes a different event from every one of these operations. Promptfoo can be the tested section showing how we retained real answer evidence for review; it should not stand in for the whole citation ecosystem.
