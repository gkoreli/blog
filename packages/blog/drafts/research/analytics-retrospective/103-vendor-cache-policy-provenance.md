## Separate storage, reuse and membership

[RFC 9111](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#section-5.2.2.4) separates validation before reuse from storage restrictions; freshness lifetime and age are separate inputs. The inspected, [commit-pinned http-cache-semantics implementation](https://github.com/kornelski/http-cache-semantics/blob/b1d4bd682fbab0252985de45219f4e7497c0067c/index.js) also separates `storable()`, `maxAge()` and `satisfiesWithoutRevalidation()`. We borrow that distinction for evidence. No dependency or source is copied. Its broader policies and duplicate-directive parser TODO are not adopted as our contract. [Source hashes and cross-references](vendor-cache-evidence-sources.json) identify the actual inspected files; an initial `master` commit lookup returns 422, then repository metadata resolves `main` before the source is pinned.

Each attempted source distinguishes fetched, revalidated, memory, coalesced, cooldown and failed paths. Policy facts contain an assessment time, a lifetime source (max-age, Expires or local default) and overlapping blockers: no-store, private, no-cache, invalid/zero lifetime, invalid age or exhausted age. Empty blockers describe positive reuse **at the accepted validation**, not current freshness or server-wide reuse.

Address and fanout guards have an explicit null cache assessment. Cooldown can carry the last accepted response's derived policy for that source and isolate, without a membership snapshot. A new attempt clears this context; failure leaves it unknown. No-store/private retain no feed body or validators; the separate diagnostic context contains only closed processing facts.
