## A hypothesis we did not turn into a new bot rule

The current lexical browser-family helper prefers Chromium over Firefox over WebKit compatibility markers. A string containing both Chromium and Firefox products therefore raises a question about which normalization is useful; it does not establish malformed HTTP or a bot. [RFC 9110 §10.1.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-10.1.5) permits multiple products and comments. [Mozilla's browser-detection guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Browser_detection_using_the_user_agent) explains compatibility tokens and spoofing, including why a Safari token alongside Chrome cannot select Safari. The captured family remains a lexical claim. No new family-conflict rule or historical reclassification is adopted from this hypothesis.

These documents constrain the interpretation; they do not independently identify the client that produced our request. The captured operator label comes from the actual controlled browser action, rather than inferred header syntax.
