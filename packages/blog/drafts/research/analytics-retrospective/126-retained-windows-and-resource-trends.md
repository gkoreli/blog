## Why this cannot become a normalized growth curve

**1,245 of 1,477 events have both method stamps null**, and every stamped event falls in the last week. Matching nulls do not prove stable rules. Recorded stamps preserve implementation provenance without authenticating a request's deployed source, changing network feeds or classifier accuracy. A Browser-selected history cannot recover events earlier rules classified elsewhere or failed to store.

The two all-kind selections contain 97 and 397 raw records, with adjacent exact-ID bounds and no duplicate IDs. They span unequal portions of October 29–30. Their collection/classifier profiles change: 77 of the first 97 use `.29.5/.29.2`, twenty use `.29.6/.29.3`; all later 397 use `.29.6/.29.3`. Four controls in the first slice are excluded. **Twenty-three raw Browser records also occur in the complete-day capture** and match twelve selected fields each. They are overlapping evidence, not another audience increment. Neither 97→397 nor 139→355 by partial calendar day is a valid growth rate.

Catalogue dates are calendar labels, not exact first-served times or verified availability intervals. Articles have unequal exposure, and five newer registry entries postdate the old traffic slice. No age-normalized rate or zero-demand claim follows. Aggregate agreement with the later native report does not refresh the earlier row-level owner-link state or prove unchanged membership.

[Matomo's annotations](https://matomo.org/guide/reporting-tools/annotations/) are a useful precedent for retaining dated context beside charts. [Google's performance-report contract](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) treats search clicks, impressions and queries as their own measurements. Our inference: keep scope, method and exposure visible, and do not manufacture search intent from page-request counts. The Plausible comparison URL attempted this turn was unavailable; it supplies no supporting claim.
