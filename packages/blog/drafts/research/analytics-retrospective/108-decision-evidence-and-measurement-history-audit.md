## A reproducible information gap in the readership report

The report already warns that rules can change, but it exposes only one distinct-classifier count for the whole window. The [paired native experiment](readership-profile-ambiguity.json) constructs the same sixteen events under the full current schema and changes only classifier stamps:

| Fixture | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed throughout | A:2, B:2 | A:2, B:2 | A:2, B:2 | A:2, B:2 |
| Switched halfway | A:4 | A:4 | B:4 | B:4 |

Both actual library reports are identical, including weekly totals, all source/article metrics and `recordedProfiles: 2`. Each local report costs 4,988 reads and zero writes. This proves loss of weekly profile distribution in the report; it does not prove that either fixture happened in production or quantify a rule's effect. Even identical stamps would not prove comparable populations or unchanged collection conditions.

[SQLite's aggregate contract](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_aggfunc.html) explains that DISTINCT removes duplicate values before counting. It does not retain their time distribution. [Matomo's annotations](https://matomo.org/guide/reporting-tools/annotations/) offer a useful precedent: attach historical context to dated reports. Our proposed extension uses retained profile counts as well as dated explanations, with no new dependency or tracking. [Cross-references](decision-audit-sources.json) separate that borrowed idea from the measured counterexample.
