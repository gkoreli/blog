# llms.txt and GEO — Evergreen Search Landscape

**Status:** Search-intent and competitive-content snapshot for the second draft  
**Observed:** 2026-08-25  
**Purpose:** Decide how the article can become a durable acquisition doorway without changing its evidence to fit a keyword.

## Constraint before the strategy

No title or outline can guarantee “a lot of traffic,” especially for a young domain. This snapshot establishes visible query language and competitive gaps, not search volume or ranking probability. We do not yet have a page-specific Search Console query export because the page does not exist. The acquisition claim must therefore remain a goal to measure over 60–90 days, not a promised outcome.

## Queries inspected

- `llms.txt GEO does it work guide 2026`
- `what is llms.txt how to implement test llms.txt`
- `generative engine optimization GEO what works evidence guide`
- `llms.txt AI search optimization evidence`

## What the result landscape says readers want

The recurring wording is practical and skeptical:

1. **Does `llms.txt` work?** Readers want a verdict, current evidence, and provider positions.
2. **What is `llms.txt`?** Readers need the root-file convention distinguished from `robots.txt`, sitemaps, page Markdown, and `llms-full.txt`.
3. **How do I create it?** Implementation results lead with the exact format, a copyable example, placement at `/llms.txt`, and validation.
4. **Does it improve AI search visibility or GEO?** Readers need search discovery/citation separated from known-site agent navigation.
5. **How should I test it?** Stronger competing pages discuss logs and measurement, but most do not expose an end-to-end implementation failure or a pre-registered task benchmark.
6. **What should I do instead?** The durable answer returns to crawlable/indexable HTML, original evidence, internal links, and provider-supported measurement.

## The competitive shapes already present

### Evidence-first negative verdict

[AI Visible](https://www.aivisible.co.uk/research/llms-txt-ai-search-evidence.html), [Joseph Timpson](https://josephtimpson.com/insights/does-llms-txt-work), and [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/blog/llms-txt-search-visibility/) all answer whether the file improves AI search. They center Google's position and Ahrefs' May 2026 request data. Their shared practical verdict is that the file has no demonstrated AI-search visibility lift and should not displace ordinary search/content work.

This means our article cannot win by merely repeating “97% got no requests” or “Google ignores it.” That answer is now crowded.

### Implementation guide

[Primores](https://primores.org/blog/how-to-create-llms-txt/) answers the creation query in four minutes: required/optional fields, a copyable template, root placement, `llms-full.txt`, curation, and a short caveat. It is easy to scan and immediately useful.

Our first draft does not yet satisfy this intent. It describes the artifacts but never gives the reader a minimal example, validation commands, or a decision path by site type.

### Broad GEO workflow

[Open Queries](https://openqueries.org/generative-engine-optimization) treats GEO as an evidence and measurement discipline layered on ordinary search eligibility. It offers a workflow, example, measurement boundaries, and quality gate.

A generic “complete GEO guide” would put gkoreli.com against well-structured specialist publications on a much broader query. Our stronger entry point is the concrete `llms.txt` question, with GEO explained only far enough to stop readers from assigning the file the wrong job.

### New observational studies

[SearchScore](https://searchscore.io/research/does-llms-txt-work/) reports a weak association between publishing the file and its own AI-readiness score, then shows that part of the gap is mechanically created by the score rewarding the file. It explicitly treats the result as motivation for a controlled follow-up, not causal proof.

This confirms that the evidence will keep changing. The article needs a dated evidence box and stable decision framework, not a title whose usefulness expires when one new dataset appears.

## Our defensible gap

The research folder contains a combination the visible results do not currently offer in one article:

- a real publisher who implemented the full stack before reaching a verdict;
- an internal architecture record preserving the original belief;
- a discovered analytics failure showing why “AI reads” often measures the wrong request;
- Cloudflare edge evidence from the actual static files;
- a purpose-aware crawler taxonomy rather than one `AI bot` bucket;
- reproduced use of this blog through LangChain `mcpdoc`;
- pinned maintained agent workflows from Google, Prismatic, tldraw, and Streamlit;
- a measured HTML-versus-Markdown representation difference;
- the original GEO paper read alongside 2026 fixed-candidate and frozen-retrieval studies;
- a pre-registered benchmark and decision thresholds.

The unique evergreen promise is therefore not “I also checked the logs.” It is:

> A builder's guide to what `llms.txt` can and cannot do across AI search, agent navigation, Markdown consumption, and measurement—with a working implementation and tests.

## Recommended query architecture

### Primary intent

**`does llms.txt work` / `llms.txt GEO`**

Answer in the title, first screen, and conclusion. The stable short answer:

> `llms.txt` has no demonstrated ranking or citation benefit for open-web AI search. It can help a configured agent navigate a known site, especially developer documentation. Keep it cheap; do not treat it as an acquisition strategy.

### Secondary intents the same page can honestly satisfy

- `what is llms.txt`
- `how to create llms.txt`
- `llms.txt example`
- `llms.txt vs robots.txt`
- `llms.txt vs sitemap`
- `llms-full.txt`
- `track AI crawlers`
- `Markdown for AI agents`
- `generative engine optimization what works`

These should be real sections, not keyword variants. Each intent corresponds to a reader decision the research already supports.

### Intent that should not govern this page

**`complete GEO guide`** is too broad and commercially crowded. The article should explain the GEO evidence because it changes the `llms.txt` decision; it should not pretend to be a complete playbook for brand mentions, third-party authority, entity consistency, shopping feeds, local search, or platform monitoring.

## Recommended article doorway

### H1

`Does llms.txt Work? What I Found After Building and Measuring It`

This carries the exact reader question and the author's earned first-person basis. It is more discoverable than `I Gave llms.txt a Smaller Job` without becoming a generic “ultimate guide.”

### SEO title

`Does llms.txt Work for GEO? An Evidence-Based Guide`

This is declarative enough to match the query and remains useful without a year. A visible `Last checked` date inside the evidence section can carry freshness without making the title expire.

### Description

`Does llms.txt improve AI search visibility? I audited real request logs, agent clients, Markdown endpoints, and GEO research—then built a practical decision guide.`

### First-screen contract

The opening should contain, in order:

1. a two- or three-sentence direct verdict;
2. one sentence establishing that Goga built and measured the system;
3. a compact “use it for / do not expect” table;
4. then the March implementation story.

The current line `I built llms.txt for a blog nobody read` should survive. It is the living center, but it should follow or share the first screen with the direct answer instead of delaying it.

## Recommended durable structure

1. **Does `llms.txt` work? The short answer** — search versus configured-agent verdict.
2. **What `llms.txt` is—and is not** — index, page Markdown, full dump, sitemap, `robots.txt`.
3. **I built the full stack, then found my metric could not see it** — first-person proof and analytics failure.
4. **The six stages hidden inside “works”** — durable analytical model.
5. **What providers and real clients do in 2026** — dated evidence table with official search positions and pinned agent workflows.
6. **How to create a useful `llms.txt`** — minimal example, generated-source rule, links to canonical pages/Markdown, validation.
7. **How to test it without lying to yourself** — `curl`, edge logs, purpose-aware UA categories, request-versus-use boundary, task benchmark.
8. **What GEO evidence actually supports** — effects after retrieval, unique evidence, relevance, and qualifications.
9. **Decision guide by site type and goal** — docs, personal blog, business/commerce, large corpus.
10. **What I am keeping on gkoreli.com** — artifact-level decision and next observation.
11. **FAQ** — only questions that close genuine ambiguity: ranking, `robots.txt`, sitemap, full dump, crawler blocking, Markdown.
12. **Dated evidence ledger/glossary** — primary and pinned sources.

## Traffic and authenticity can reinforce each other

The search-facing promise is a question Goga genuinely needed answered. The distinctive material is the implementation error and the client reproduction, not a manufactured contrarian opinion. Practical sections should be derived from those receipts:

- the example comes from the generator already running;
- the tracking guide comes from discovering that browser analytics cannot observe static retrieval;
- the site-type table comes from real clients giving different artifacts different jobs;
- the GEO advice comes from study-design limits, not a checklist of magic formatting tactics.

This is evergreen writing in Goga's voice: answer a recurring question, then show the engineering work that made the answer trustworthy.

## Measurement after publication

Traffic is the goal, but the article needs a falsifiable acquisition test:

- establish the page's indexed/canonical state;
- request indexing once after publication and after material revisions;
- after fresh data exists, export page-filtered Search Console queries;
- bucket them into `llms.txt implementation`, `does it work`, `GEO`, `AI crawler measurement`, and bad-fit intents;
- measure impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position separately by bucket at 30, 60, and 90 days;
- do not rewrite the article repeatedly while waiting for recrawl;
- tune the title/description only when query data exposes a doorway mismatch;
- add or split a companion page only when real queries show an intent the article cannot serve cleanly.

Relevant traffic—not raw visits—is the success metric. A page that attracts people seeking a one-click “GEO hack” and disappoints them is not a win for the publication.
