# llms.txt and GEO — Green-Team Editorial Opportunity

**Status:** Editorial strategy artifact. Not article prose.  
**Created:** 2026-08-25  
**Purpose:** Follow the thesis red-team with a constructive pass: preserve every evidence boundary while finding the strongest useful version of the article, the real opportunity inside the convention, and an update path if the evidence changes.  
**Inputs:** The full `llms-txt-geo` research folder, Goga's golden prompts, the evergreen content-mix artifact, and the `shape-article`, `blog-writing`, and `shareable-engineering` skills.

## Green-team mandate

The red-team asked, “Where does this thesis outrun the evidence?” The green-team asks a different question:

> Given what survived, what useful system, decision rule, and shared investigation can we build?

Green-teaming does not reverse the red-team or search for an optimistic spin. It adapts to the narrower evidence and looks for opportunity inside it. The aim is to prevent skepticism from becoming the article's whole personality.

The constructive move is not from “`llms.txt` works” to “`llms.txt` is dead.” It is from one oversized job to several smaller, testable jobs:

- organic discovery and selection;
- navigation inside a known site;
- clean page consumption;
- task performance after the entry point is supplied;
- measurement of requests, source use, citations, and human referrals as separate events.

Once those jobs are separated, the article has more to offer than a debunking. It can show readers where a low-cost convention is already useful, how to implement it proportionally, how to measure it honestly, and what new evidence would justify investing more.

## The strongest constructive thesis

### Editorial thesis

> `llms.txt` does not have to create an audience to earn a small job. Its strongest demonstrated role is to give an agent that already knows a site a concise map to useful, page-level content. That makes it worth keeping when it is generated from canonical sources, cheap to review, explicitly discoverable to the clients that matter, and tested on real tasks. GEO's durable opportunity is not a formatting trick; it is making unique evidence relevant, self-contained, and usable after retrieval while ordinary search eligibility still does the earlier work.

### Plain-language version

> Keep `llms.txt` small, give it a real job, and measure that job. Do not ask it to manufacture demand.

### Positive disagreeable claim

> The practical value of `llms.txt` is optionality, not traffic: a cheap, generated interface for agents is worth maintaining before broad adoption only when its cost stays close to zero and its success criteria stay bounded.

This is positive without becoming promotional. It says there is a rational reason to ship the convention now, but only under a cost cap and an explicit job.

### Falsifiers and expanders

The position is deliberately changeable.

- It weakens if generated files drift from canonical content, become a security or maintenance burden, or fail to help real agent tasks.
- It strengthens if multiple independent clients use the map-and-Markdown path, the controlled benchmark shows quality-preserving efficiency gains, or provider guidance begins recommending it for a named retrieval stage.
- It changes category if a search provider documents or demonstrates organic discovery or selection lift from the file. At that point it is no longer only known-site navigation.

## Governing question

> If `llms.txt` cannot create the reason an agent enters my site, what useful job can I give it today—and what evidence would earn it a larger job tomorrow?

This question is better than “Does `llms.txt` work?” because the latter hides the job, client, and measurement. It also gives the article forward motion after the measurement failure.

## Living center, preserved and extended

The existing living center remains load-bearing:

> Goga built a complete AI-readable layer for a blog that still has almost no recurring audience. He now has to find out whether he built an access path for real agents, a speculative search ritual, or both—and whether his own analytics can even tell the difference.

The green-team extension is:

> Finding that the system cannot prove audience growth does not make the build meaningless. It forces Goga to give each artifact a smaller job, repair the observation boundary, and decide what evidence would make him invest again.

This protects the personal stake without turning the lack of audience into bait. Wanting readers remains true. The article's constructive achievement is learning how not to confuse readiness for agents with demand from readers.

## Governing form and movement

**Governing form:** Research synthesis with a first-person engineering field investigation.

**Why it fits:** The evidence can support a bounded engineering decision, while the first-person implementation and measurement failure keep it from becoming a generic market survey.

### Movement

1. **Hope:** I built an AI-readable layer because I wanted the blog to be findable and usable.
2. **Failure:** The number named “AI Reads” cannot observe the resource requests the layer was built for.
3. **Separation:** Discovery, selection, navigation, consumption, citation, and referral are different events.
4. **Recovery:** The proposal's smaller known-site navigation job is real, and one maintained Google agent workflow demonstrates it.
5. **Adaptation:** I assign separate jobs to the index, page Markdown, full dump, provider crawler controls, and measurement system.
6. **Action:** I keep low-cost optionality, put the full dump on trial, repair measurement semantics, and pre-register a task benchmark.
7. **Invitation:** I publish the method and ask for reproducible traces or counterexamples that can change the next version.

### Honest ending

End at a revised operating decision plus an open evidence contract:

- what stays;
- what is on probation;
- what will be measured next;
- what result would change the decision;
- what kind of outside evidence is useful.

Do not end with “GEO is fake,” “content is king,” or a conversion CTA. The article should end more capable of changing its mind than it began.

## The positive opportunity the red-team leaves standing

### 1. A lightweight agent interface can be valuable without being a ranking signal

The small index and page-level Markdown form an interface over content the blog already maintains. When generated from the same source as HTML, their marginal publishing cost is low. This gives Goga optionality if more coding agents adopt the convention, without requiring a speculative editorial workflow.

The positive claim is not “future standards always win.” It is “cheap interoperability bets are reasonable when they remain cheap.”

### 2. Developer documentation gives the convention a concrete beachhead

The pinned Gemini skill is not merely proof that some file was fetched. It demonstrates a coherent workflow:

1. an agent has a known documentation domain and a task;
2. it reads a concise index;
3. it follows links to targeted Markdown;
4. it avoids loading an entire site.

That pattern is useful for framework and tool documentation, including future documentation for `@nisli/core`, `ghx`, `backlog-mcp`, or AgentPort. The blog itself may be a weak discovery use case while the author's project documentation becomes a strong navigation use case. The article should name that difference as an adaptation, not a contradiction.

### 3. Page-level Markdown may be more valuable than the famous root file

The market conversation centers the filename. The practical product may be the path behind it: clean, canonical, page-level content fetched on demand.

This creates a useful distinction for readers:

- `llms.txt` is the map;
- page Markdown is the destination;
- `llms-full.txt` is a separate bulk-transfer choice;
- ordinary HTML remains the public canonical document and discovery surface.

The controlled benchmark can test whether the map and destinations reduce navigation or transfer cost without reducing answer quality. This is a real engineering question even if search rankings never move.

### 4. GEO research still has a constructive lesson

The newer evidence does not make post-retrieval optimization imaginary. It makes it conditional. Once a source enters a candidate set, relevance, content substance, context position, and presentation can affect use and citation.

The practical lesson is stronger than a list of tricks:

- publish information that is unique enough to deserve selection;
- answer a defined question rather than spraying related terms;
- preserve exact numbers, qualifications, and provenance;
- make a claim self-contained enough to survive extraction;
- structure the source so both a person and an agent can recover the argument;
- avoid decorative statistics, quotation stuffing, and machine-only summaries.

These practices are already compatible with Goga's builder-journal voice. GEO does not need to become an alien editorial persona.

### 5. The measurement failure creates a better reusable contribution

Many articles can repeat provider bot names. Fewer can show how an analytics architecture produced a convincing metric for the wrong event.

The constructive contribution is a reusable measurement model:

- browser page views are not crawler requests;
- search crawlers are not training crawlers;
- user-directed fetchers are not automatic indexers;
- requests are not source use;
- source use is not citation;
- citation is not a human referral.

Readers leave able to audit their own “AI traffic” dashboards. That value exists regardless of whether they adopt `llms.txt`.

### 6. A public, pre-registered experiment can create contact rather than manufactured engagement

The benchmark and edge observation plan allow the article to remain alive after publication. Goga can expose the protocol, negative results, contaminated windows, and update triggers. That invites engineers to contribute comparable evidence instead of opinions.

The desired interaction is not “agree with my verdict.” It is “show me a trace, client, or task result that changes this table.”

## Constructive operating model: be ready, do not bet the blog

The green strategy is an option-value strategy.

### Keep

- A concise, generated `llms.txt` index sourced from canonical post metadata.
- Page-level Markdown generated from the same content pipeline.
- Indexable semantic HTML as the canonical reader and search surface.
- Provider-documented crawler access where it matches the author's policy.
- Dated source audits and explicit evidence boundaries.

### Test

- Whether map-plus-Markdown improves real tasks over HTML in the pre-registered benchmark.
- Whether v2 `alternate` and `describedby` relations produce observable follow-on navigation after a clean baseline.
- Whether edge logs show named or generic agents requesting the files over a declared window.
- Whether Bing's citation telemetry exposes any useful page/query patterns.

### Put on probation

- The 375 KB full dump. Retain it only if cross-post synthesis benefits justify the payload and maintenance surface.
- The public “AI Reads” metric name. It should not survive once the article establishes that it counts client beacon events.
- A single `Human | Bot | AI` classifier. It hides the very distinctions the investigation needs.

### Do not invest in yet

- Manually curated duplicate prose for machines.
- Keyword- or quotation-stuffing sold as GEO.
- A bespoke `llms.txt` editorial calendar.
- Claims about organic lift without provider evidence or controlled retrieval-inclusive measurement.
- A giant evaluation program before the small task benchmark proves useful.

## Constructive reader decision matrix

| Reader situation | Useful job for the stack | Minimum sensible action | Evidence to collect | Investment ceiling now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal blog with little agent traffic | Optional machine-readable access after a known visit | Keep canonical HTML healthy; auto-generate a small index and per-page Markdown only if maintenance stays negligible | Edge requests, chat referrals, search/citation telemetry | Low; do not expect audience creation |
| Developer documentation with coding-agent users | Fast navigation from a known docs domain to exact pages | Small curated map, targeted Markdown, v2 link relations, explicit agent instructions where appropriate | Task accuracy, fetched bytes/tokens, navigation traces, citation correctness | Moderate when benchmarked use exists |
| Product with an agent skill or MCP integration | Stable content interface the client can deliberately call | Name the index in the client/skill and version the expected format | Reproducible client traces and failure rates | Proportional to real client adoption |
| Large documentation site | Reduce broad crawling and improve targeted consumption | Sectioned index plus page alternatives; avoid one giant context dump by default | Crawl paths, transfer volume, freshness failures, task completion | Increase only when operational savings appear |
| Private RAG or internal corpus | Controlled navigation and self-contained source material | Evaluate page structure and linked context inside the actual retrieval pipeline | Retrieval recall, answer grounding, latency, truncation, access control | Case-specific; open-web results do not transfer automatically |
| Publisher seeking Google AI visibility | Eligibility and source selection, not `llms.txt` | Crawlable/indexable HTML, internal links, unique evidence, Search Console | Index state, query fit, citations/referrals where exposed | Spend on content and ordinary discoverability first |

The matrix is the article's main practical deliverable. It arms the reader without pretending one configuration fits every client.

## Article section architecture and each section's job

These are section functions, not drafted headings or prose.

### 1. The layer I built for an audience I did not have

**Job:** Establish the honest want, the March causal model, and the actual implementation. Name the small audience without converting it into a dramatic hook.

**Protected material:** The desire for readers; the old ADR; the generated index, full dump, page Markdown, JSON-LD, and analytics loop.

**Reader gain:** A concrete system, not a trend explainer.

### 2. The most convincing number was measuring another event

**Job:** Reveal the `ai_fetches` category error through code and architecture.

**Protected material:** The client beacon, asset-first routing, missing static requests, and the difference between a browser event and a resource GET.

**Reader gain:** A method for auditing their own telemetry.

### 3. “Does it work?” hides six different questions

**Job:** Expand the current three-stage model where needed into discovery, indexing/eligibility, retrieval/selection, navigation, use/citation, and referral.

**Protected material:** Do not let a request count travel to another stage.

**Reader gain:** A diagnostic model they can reuse before buying GEO advice.

### 4. The proposal's smallest job is its best one

**Job:** Explain the index-plus-targeted-Markdown design generously. Distinguish it from the `robots.txt` analogy and from a ranking file.

**Positive evidence:** The v2 design, maintainer account, Chrome's optional audit, and generated low marginal cost.

**Reader gain:** Understand what to build and why, without a straw man.

### 5. One real Google agent uses this path

**Job:** Present the Gemini skill early enough that skepticism does not dominate the article. Walk through the known-domain documentation workflow and bound it precisely.

**Reader gain:** A concrete use case and a clue about which sites benefit most.

### 6. The index, Markdown pages, and full dump are different products

**Job:** Stop discussing the stack as one checkbox. Assign each artifact a job, cost, and test.

**Reader gain:** A more useful implementation decision than “add or do not add `llms.txt`.”

### 7. GEO begins after another system already chose the source

**Job:** Explain the 2024–2026 evidence without reducing it to either “40% lift” or “nothing works.” Show the fixed candidate sets, then extract the constructive lesson about relevance, substance, and recoverable evidence.

**Reader gain:** A writing standard that improves human trust and can improve source use without changing voice.

### 8. The stack I would build now

**Job:** Give the current decisions: keep the generated map and Markdown, preserve HTML, test v2 relations after baseline, put the full dump on probation, repair event semantics, and keep investment proportional.

**Reader gain:** The constructive decision matrix and a staged implementation order.

### 9. The experiment that can change my mind

**Job:** Present E1 and E2 compactly, including their limits. State the thresholds that would expand or shrink investment.

**Reader gain:** A reproducible method rather than a testimonial.

### 10. Bring evidence, not allegiance

**Job:** Invite collaboration around traces, task benchmarks, provider changes, and counterexamples. Own the desire for contact without asking for engagement as a metric.

**Reader gain:** A way to participate that can materially update the article.

## Title architecture

### Recommended H1

`I Gave llms.txt a Smaller Job`

Why it works:

- It expresses movement and adaptation rather than final dismissal.
- It is first-person and could only come from someone who shipped the layer.
- It creates curiosity without implying that no agent uses the file.
- It leaves room for the measurement story and the constructive decision.

Risk: `llms.txt` is visible, but “GEO” is not. Use the `seoTitle`, description, and orientation sentence for that handle.

### Strong alternatives

- `I Built llms.txt for the Wrong Job` — clearest confession and strongest conflict; slightly harsher than the final constructive position.
- `llms.txt Won't Find You an Audience. It Can Still Help Agents.` — highly legible decision; more declarative and less personal.
- `What llms.txt Is Good For` — constructive and evergreen; too generic unless the first-person implementation appears immediately.
- `I Made My Blog Agent-Readable. Here Is What That Can Do.` — positive and grounded; longer and less sharp.
- `llms.txt Is Not a Ranking Signal. That Doesn't Make It Useless.` — strong stage distinction; risks sounding like a generic rebuttal article.

### Recommended metadata layer

- **seoTitle:** `llms.txt and GEO: What Works and How to Test It`
- **alternativeHeadline:** `llms.txt, page-level Markdown, AI crawler measurement, and generative engine optimization`
- **description:** `I audited my blog's llms.txt, Markdown endpoints, AI crawler analytics, and GEO evidence—then gave each part a smaller, testable job.`
- **orientation sentence job:** Name the shipped files, the broken “AI Reads” metric, and the investigation. Do not state the verdict before the reader sees the failure.

Avoid a year in the H1. Put the evidence check date visibly near the source appendix and use `lastModified` only when the article's evidence, decisions, or experiments materially change.

## Authenticity: why this remains Goga's article

The article stays genuine because the useful advice emerges from a system Goga built and a belief he has to revise.

### Keep visible

- He wanted readers and believed machine readability might help.
- The implementation was not fake work; it was a reasonable cheap bet built from an incomplete causal model.
- The metric name let hope travel farther than the underlying event.
- Research produced a narrower use case, not a triumphant debunking.
- The decision is adaptive: keep cheap optionality, spend only after evidence.

### Do not perform

- Indifference to traffic.
- Embarrassment for having tried the proposal.
- Hostility toward Jeremy Howard, GEO researchers, SEO practitioners, or providers.
- Certainty about opaque systems.
- A conversion from believer to skeptic as a personal brand.

### What gives the article warmth

- Treat the old ADR as a preserved model, not evidence of foolishness.
- Praise the proposal for the job it is good at.
- Let the Gemini skill be a genuine surprise, not a caveat buried after pages of criticism.
- Admit that low-cost readiness has value even before traffic arrives.
- Let the article end with curiosity and a method for learning, not superiority.

## Collaboration without engagement farming

The article should invite specific evidence that can alter the artifact.

### Useful invitations

- **Agent/client maintainers:** Share a reproducible client trace showing when the client discovers or follows `llms.txt`, including client/version, initial entry point, requested paths, and task outcome.
- **Documentation publishers:** Share anonymized path, claimed user agent, status, and follow-on request counts for a declared observation window. A zero is useful.
- **Researchers:** Point to retrieval-inclusive experiments or isolate the contribution of the root index from page Markdown and bundled structured content.
- **Readers with a real task:** Run or adapt the frozen HTML vs map-plus-Markdown benchmark and return the raw protocol and traces, not only the winner.
- **Providers:** Link to explicit current documentation if a product's treatment of the file changes.

### Unhelpful invitations

- “Do you use `llms.txt`?” without client, task, or trace.
- Votes on whether GEO is real.
- Screenshots of a single answer without the query, date, provider, and source path.
- Unverifiable ranking or citation uplift percentages.
- Generic requests to like, share, subscribe, or “join the conversation.”

### Contact metric

The success signal is one piece of evidence that updates the decision table, benchmark, provider matrix, or client-use map. Page views and agreement are secondary.

## Flexible update system

The article should be designed for revision without making every conclusion feel temporary.

### Stable layer

Keep these concepts in the main argument:

- different stages require different evidence;
- a request is not a citation or referral;
- the client and task determine whether a format is useful;
- source substance and relevance are more durable than a formatting ritual;
- low-cost optionality deserves a cost ceiling and a falsifier.

### Volatile layer

Keep these in a dated table or glossary so they can change locally:

- provider crawler identifiers and purposes;
- provider statements about `llms.txt`;
- proposal version and link relations;
- named clients that consume the file;
- request observations and benchmark results;
- file sizes and blog catalog counts.

### Update triggers

| Trigger | What it would change | Required evidence | Article action |
|---|---|---|---|
| A search provider documents `llms.txt` use for organic retrieval or ranking | Expands the file beyond known-site navigation | First-party provider documentation or reproducible product behavior | Update provider table, thesis scope, and decision matrix; set material `lastModified` |
| Two or more independent maintained clients deliberately consume it | Strengthens interoperability and docs use | Pinned client code/instructions plus reproducible path | Add client map and raise the docs recommendation |
| E2 shows map-plus-Markdown is quality-preserving and at least 20% more efficient across more than one agent | Strengthens the consumption case | Frozen protocol, raw traces, blind scoring, declared threshold | Publish results and recommend the pattern for matching tasks |
| E2 shows no material gain or worse grounding | Weakens the stack's task-value claim | Same protocol and raw results | Keep only if marginal cost is negligible; simplify or remove redundant endpoints |
| Full dump improves cross-post synthesis enough to offset its payload | Gives `llms-full.txt` a bounded job | Condition C results with task, quality, and transfer measures | Retain for that use case and document size constraints |
| Full dump adds cost without task gain | Removes a speculative artifact | Condition C results | Deprecate or stop generating it; record the decision |
| Clean edge window shows repeated map → Markdown request sequences | Strengthens observed navigation use | Non-contaminated path/time aggregates; identity stated as claimed or verified | Add observation, not a citation claim; consider longer measurement |
| V2 relations precede a sustained navigation change | Makes local discoverability worth further testing | Baseline and intervention windows with no competing change | Report association, retain relations, avoid causal language unless design supports it |
| Named crawler identifiers or policies change | Changes classification and eligibility guidance | Current primary provider docs | Update volatile table and classifier artifact |
| A prompt-injection or staleness failure appears | Raises the maintenance/trust cost | Reproducible incident or security advisory | Tighten generation/review and client trust guidance; reconsider option value |
| Bing/Search Console exposes meaningful citation/query data for the blog | Adds selection/citation evidence | Export with date and measurement definition | Add a distinct evidence layer without merging it into request counts |

## Evidence hierarchy for future changes

Prefer, in order:

1. Reproduced first-party behavior from this blog or benchmark.
2. Pinned client code that explicitly consumes the artifact.
3. First-party provider documentation describing a named stage.
4. Peer-reviewed experiments whose retrieval setup is stated.
5. Large observational logs with a denominator and sampling limits.
6. Maintainer reports with their scope named.
7. Anecdotes as leads for tests, not verdicts.

This lets the article stay flexible without becoming credulous whenever a new screenshot circulates.

## What the reader should be able to do afterward

The article succeeds if a reader can:

- identify which stage they are trying to improve;
- decide whether their site resembles a personal blog, developer-doc site, agent product, or private corpus;
- choose between a small index, page Markdown, and a full dump instead of shipping all three by reflex;
- inspect whether their analytics sees the original resource request;
- separate search, training, user-directed fetches, citations, and referrals;
- cap investment until a real client or task justifies more;
- design one test that could change their decision;
- submit a reproducible counterexample rather than an opinion.

## Recommended editorial decisions now

1. Keep the governing form and measurement failure from the current scratchpad.
2. Replace the dominant “bounded verdict” movement with “job reassignment and adaptive investment.”
3. Use `I Gave llms.txt a Smaller Job` as the leading H1 candidate and keep a concrete `seoTitle` for cold readers.
4. Move the official Gemini skill earlier than the Ahrefs adoption study so the proposal receives its strongest positive case before its limits.
5. Make the index / page Markdown / full dump split the practical center of the second half.
6. Present GEO research as a conditional opportunity to improve source use, while rejecting portable formatting recipes.
7. End with the pre-registered experiment and a request for reproducible evidence that can change the article.
8. Do not wait for proof of organic traffic before drafting. The article's present-tense contribution is the corrected model, the repaired decision rules, and the test design. The missing blocker is Goga's first-person memory of the original hope and what the “AI Reads” number meant to him.

## Green-team conclusion

The most useful article is not a verdict on whether `llms.txt` lives or dies. It is a field report about assigning the right job to an uncertain interface.

The red-team found that the file cannot carry the weight of audience discovery. The green-team finds that it does not need to. A generated map plus targeted Markdown can be a rational, low-cost interface for known-site agents, especially in developer documentation. The blog can keep that option open while ordinary search and original work create the reason to arrive, while instrumentation measures the right events, and while real tasks decide whether the interface deserves more investment.

That stance is useful, open to evidence, and recognizably Goga's: build the thing, inspect the boundary, admit what the metric cannot say, shrink the claim, keep the promising part, and invite other engineers to help make the next decision better.
