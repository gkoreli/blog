## Generation prompt

Create one polished, visually striking square editorial infographic for a software engineer's X post. It must feel like an exceptional contemporary technology magazine poster, funny and a little provocative, yet technically rigorous. Beautiful dark charcoal/near-black background, warm ivory oversized typography, vivid mint-green for Linux VM, warm coral-red for macOS, subtle precise technical linework, generous negative space, strong visual hierarchy. A stylish minimal illustration of one Mac computer with a Linux VM window inside it, a small charming penguin in the VM, makes clear there is ONE physical computer, not two separate machines. No photographic people. No generic corporate dashboard, no glassmorphism, no purple gradients, no lorem ipsum. All text crisply rendered and legible on a phone. Keep the strongest headline in the top third. EXACT headline text, large: "My Mac lost to the Linux VM inside it." Under it, clearly visible: "At installing dependencies." Add a small clear kicker: "SAME M5 MAC. DIFFERENT OS." Then a beautifully designed comparison with clear column headings "macOS" in coral and "Linux VM" in green. Four rows of data, exact spellings and values, seconds must be explicit: "T3 Code" | "8.12 s" | "2.10 s"; "Next.js" | "4.56 s" | "1.56 s"; "Vite" | "1.82 s" | "0.48 s"; "SvelteKit" | "0.59 s" | "0.13 s". Make T3 Code especially prominent as the lead result without hiding the other three. Use a clean numerical comparison or four paired linear bars with shared truthful scale if space allows; never distort bar lengths to exaggerate the ratio and never let an illustration obscure numbers. A compact resource comparison in two clearly labelled chips: "Mac: 10 logical CPUs / 24 GB RAM" and "VM: 4 vCPUs / 4 GB RAM". Readable footer with exact meaning, use these exact lines: "Warm-cache pnpm installs. Five-run medians." and "Install time only. Versions and fixes differ by repo." and "Not a full-app speed test or an APFS-only comparison." Small but visible credit "Goga Koreli · gkoreli.com". Prioritize elegant composition and exact data over decoration. All measured values must appear exactly once in the comparison and correspond to the correct OS. No claim that Linux is universally faster, no fabricated benchmarks, no claims of 8x performance. The headline is about this measured machine and this specific task. Deliver a final high-quality square infographic, not a mockup of a poster in a room.
