An engineering article cannot teach by describing only the mistake. For every load-bearing failure, readers must be able to reconstruct: what existed before; what broke and why; what exists now; how the current mechanism works; why that repair was chosen over alternatives; what tradeoff remains; which bounded tenet transfers; and what future vision or unresolved edge follows. Present tense includes the current implementation, rationale, and judgment—not only present pain. The article need not use rigid headings, but failure-only critique is incomplete growth-in-public writing.
