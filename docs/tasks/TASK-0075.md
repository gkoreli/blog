# TASK-0064 — Content-first article discovery positioning

## Result

A durable workflow should reverse-engineer a publication doorway from the finished article rather than retrofit an article to a keyword. Its unit of work is a **doorway package**: one primary reader/search job, a small secondary phrase set, H1, optional `seoTitle`, description, visible standfirst/orientation, honest headings, and a post-publication learning plan. It optimizes the accuracy and legibility of the invitation. It cannot guarantee impressions, rankings, clicks, links, or readers.

This skill must not choose the living center or governing form. `shape-article` does that first; `personal-essays` owns voice and unresolved experience; `blog-writing` owns structure and sourcing; `shareable-engineering` owns its existing evidence-backed trust/share mechanics. This skill uniquely owns **content-to-intent translation and doorway design**.

## What the evidence supports

1. Google defines user intent as what a person is trying to accomplish and warns that a query may have several interpretations and intents. Its useful top-level classes are Know, Know Simple, Do, Website, and Visit-in-Person; many queries do not fit one class cleanly. Know queries explicitly include broad exploration, opinions, and perspectives from real people. The rater guide is a model for reasoning about needs, not a ranking recipe; individual ratings do not move pages. [S1]
2. Google says title links help users decide what to click. Authors can influence, not control, them. Google may draw from `<title>`, the visible title/H1, `og:title`, prominent text, page text, and inbound/outbound anchor text. Titles should be descriptive, concise, accurate, unique, and not stuffed; there is no fixed title-length limit, and recrawl/reprocessing can take days to weeks. [S2]
3. Google primarily creates snippets from page content and may use the meta description when it better describes the page. Snippets can vary by query. Descriptions should be page-specific, accurate summaries rather than keyword strings; there is no fixed description-length limit, only device-dependent truncation. [S3]
4. Google recommends anticipating both novice and expert search language, but says its language matching can relate a page to queries even when exact variants are absent. Headings chiefly organize and help readers navigate. Search Essentials recommends using the words people use in prominent, descriptive places, not repeating a target phrase mechanically. [S4, S5]
5. Google explicitly ignores `<meta name=keywords>` for indexing and ranking. A phrase inventory is therefore an editorial/semantic aid, not a density target or hidden ranking lever. [S6]
6. Search Console exposes page-filterable query, click, impression, CTR, and average-position data, but results are time/place/device/history dependent. Data normally lags 2–3 days, rare/private queries are omitted, tables store top rows rather than all rows, and totals can differ by aggregation. Empty or sparse query data is not proof of no demand. [S7, S8]
7. Google’s current Search Console task guidance recommends grouping similar exact queries with regex, examining low-CTR pages, comparing periods, and focusing more on impressions/click trends than position alone. It also warns that page changes are not clean causal evidence because sentiment, news, competitors, and other events move at the same time. [S9]
8. GOV.UK content design starts from a user’s task, evidence, language, problems, and acceptance criteria. Its `As a / I need to / So that` structure is useful here if treated as a reader-job hypothesis rather than as authority over an essay’s form. [S10]
9. Nielsen Norman Group’s practitioner rule, “a link is a promise,” supplies the cold-reader test: the label creates an expectation, and the landing page must immediately confirm and fulfill it. [S11]
10. SearchPilot’s controlled page-split experiments show why title formulas should remain hypotheses. Extra near-duplicate keywords were inconclusive; airport-code jargon produced a reported 16% traffic loss; a cost question produced a reported >5% uplift; accurate dynamic prices outperformed stale static prices. The experiments measure organic traffic, combining rank and CTR effects, and SearchPilot itself says results vary by site and industry. Do not universalize any winning wording. [S12]
11. Google Trends is sampled, normalized relative interest, filters low-volume terms, adds statistical noise, and is not a perfect mirror of search activity. Autocomplete is personalized and policy-filtered and is not a popularity ranking. Use either to compare language or generate hypotheses, never as proof of absolute demand. [S13]

## Method

### 0. Lock the editorial truth before discovery work

Record these inputs from `shape-article` and the governing skill:

- governing form and honest ending;
- living center in one sentence;
- protected title/passages/phrasing;
- whether the outcome is resolved, bounded, or still live;
- the article’s portfolio role: **broad reader growth**, **narrow identity/voice**, or **contextual hybrid**.

A doorway may reveal the article more clearly. It may not choose a more marketable center, manufacture a lesson, convert inquiry into a tutorial, or make a narrow identity piece impersonate a broad guide.

### 1. Build the content inventory

Extract only what the page actually contains:

- **Entities:** named tools, projects, people, standards, concepts, versions, dates.
- **Problems:** observed friction, disputed belief, failure, decision, wound, or question.
- **Mechanisms:** causal process, architecture, workflow, comparison axis, or explanation the article demonstrates.
- **Outcomes:** measured results, decisions, changed beliefs, present state, or explicitly unresolved result. Mark each `measured`, `observed`, `inferred`, or `open`.
- **Reader value:** decision rule, trap avoided, method, evidence, sharper question, mirror, or faithful account. Different governing forms earn different value.
- **Non-promises:** likely adjacent needs the article does not meet (installation guide, benchmark, universal recommendation, final answer, etc.).
- **Exact language:** author terms, expert terms, plain-language equivalents, reader phrases already observed.

The entity/problem/mechanism/outcome inventory is the only allowed raw material for the doorway package.

### 2. Map reader awareness and search jobs

Do not force a funnel. Map plausible language states:

| Reader state | Typical job | Query grammar | Article fit test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symptom-aware | Name or explain a problem | `[symptom]`, `why [problem]` | Does the article diagnose this exact problem? |
| Mechanism-aware | Understand how/why it happens | `how [entity] works`, `[mechanism] explained` | Is the mechanism actually shown, not merely mentioned? |
| Option-aware | Compare choices or trade-offs | `[A] vs [B]`, `[tool] alternatives`, `when to use [A]` | Does it compare on the axes implied, and praise boundaries honestly? |
| Implementation-ready | Complete a task | `how to [action]`, `[entity] setup` | Can a reader actually perform the task from this page? If not, reject. |
| Evidence-seeking | Validate a claim or outcome | `[entity] benchmark`, `[method] results`, `does [X] work` | Are method, numbers, limits, and counterevidence present? |
| Named-work/identity | Find a person, project, phrase, or perspective | `[project] [concept]`, `[author] [idea]` | Is narrow recognition the honest role of the piece? |

Then write each candidate as:

`As a [specific reader in a specific state], I need to [search job], so that [decision/question/contact], and this article can satisfy it because [content evidence].`

Google’s Know/Do/Website taxonomy is a cross-check, not the primary editorial model. Most gkoreli.com articles serve complex Know, open-ended Do/research, or named-work intents; a perspective article does not need to answer a Know Simple query.

### 3. Collect query language with an evidence ladder

For every phrase, record source and confidence:

1. **Direct contact:** reader replies, corrections, issue language, community questions, author’s own problem statement.
2. **First-party observed search:** page-filtered Search Console queries for an existing or closely related post.
3. **Primary ecosystem language:** official docs, repository issues, package/readme terminology, standards.
4. **Comparative tools:** Google Trends term/topic comparison, only when volume is sufficient.
5. **SERP/autocomplete/PAA:** hypothesis about wording or result shape only; never a demand count. Inspect the result types and conflicting interpretations, not just repeated words.

Decompose useful phrases as `[entity/topic] + [job/problem] + [qualifier/context] + [outcome/constraint]`. Keep plain and expert variants when both audiences are legitimate. Reject phrases whose implied format or outcome the article does not deliver.

### 4. Select one primary doorway

Choose one reader-state + job pair. A primary doorway is not necessarily the largest query; it is the largest **honest** overlap among content, living center, reader value, and available language evidence. Keep 2–5 secondary phrases that describe genuine subtopics or adjacent phrasings already present in the article. They are semantic coverage, not keyword quotas.

Portfolio modes:

- **Broad reader-growth:** prefer the clearest well-supported problem/job and concrete handles.
- **Narrow identity/voice:** preserve the literary H1 and precise description; search may be a secondary doorway or no meaningful doorway yet. Distribution and reader contact can be primary.
- **Hybrid:** preserve the voice-led H1 while `seoTitle`, description, and standfirst expose the technical/problem handles.

Never make every piece carry all three roles.

### 5. Generate a coherent surface package

Generate 3 packages, score them, and keep one. Do not mix the most optimized line from each candidate if the resulting promise changes across surfaces.

- **H1 / `og:title`:** the real reader-facing title. Preserve protected/literary language when that is the article.
- **`seoTitle` / HTML `<title>`:** optional cold-search label. Add only when H1 lacks enough concrete handles. It should share the H1’s substance, front-load the most diagnostic entity/problem, and remain accurate if truncated. Google has no hard character limit; the existing 40–60-character heuristic is a starting preview range, not a gate.
- **Description:** one standalone page-specific sentence: `[entity/problem] + [what the page does] + [bounded value/current state]`. Do not state the thesis as settled when the article does not. Expect Google may replace it per query.
- **Standfirst or orientation:** one visible, natural sentence early in the page naming the cast and the story. It can state a finding, live question, or current state. Do not add both a standfirst and orientation when one would do, and do not bolt an abstract onto an exposed essay whose opening already orients the reader.
- **`alternativeHeadline`:** explicit semantic label when useful to the project’s JSON-LD contract; not a hidden ranking field.
- **Headings:** each section label should answer “what changes or becomes available here?” Use concrete entities/mechanisms where natural. Preserve literary or fragmentary headings when the governing form requires them. No repeated target phrase, no invented FAQ, no heading added solely for a keyword.
- **Tags/phrase set:** 2–5 honest concepts for site organization and semantic labeling. `<meta name=keywords>` is forbidden/weightless for Google.
- **Internal cross-reference:** link only for predecessor, sequel, correction, deeper dive, or product-boundary relationship, with one contextual sentence and root-relative anchor text that describes the destination. Tags alone do not justify a link. Follow the repository’s series trail rules.

Useful title structures, not formulas: `[entity] + [mechanism/outcome]`; `[problem] + [evidence/trade-off]`; or `[first-person tension] + [concrete entity]`. The best structure is the one the content inventory can cash.

### 6. Run the promise-content verification

For every meaningful noun, verb, number, comparison, and outcome in the package, point to the passage that fulfills it. Then run a cold-reader simulation:

1. From title + description alone, what page type and payoff would a stranger expect?
2. Do the first 100 words confirm the entity, problem, and epistemic state?
3. Can the relevant section heading lead them to the promised material?
4. Does the ending fulfill the job in the way the governing form allows?
5. List every likely false expectation. Remove or narrow the phrase that caused it.

Hard vetoes: invented outcome; unsupported comparison; tutorial wording without executable guidance; benchmark wording without method/limits; resolved wording for an open question; H1/seoTitle mismatch severe enough that Google or a reader would reasonably choose a different title; or damage to protected voice/living center.

## Scoring rubric

Apply hard vetoes first. Then score each dimension 0–4 and compute `sum(weight × score/4)`.

| Dimension | Weight | 4 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Promise-content fit | 30 | Every meaningful promise is fulfilled with explicit evidence and correct limits |
| Living-center/form preservation | 20 | Package clarifies without resolving, flattening, or displacing the governing form |
| Reader-job clarity | 15 | One specific reader can tell what this page helps them do/understand |
| Concrete/query-language handles | 15 | Diagnostic entities and natural reader language appear without stuffing or jargon-only phrasing |
| Cross-surface coherence | 10 | H1, seoTitle, description, standfirst, headings, and anchors describe the same page while serving their audiences |
| Distinctiveness and human voice | 10 | The package is specific to this article, non-boilerplate, and still sounds like this publication |

Acceptance: ≥80/100, with Promise-content fit and Living-center preservation each ≥3. A lower-scoring narrow identity piece is not “fixed” by broadening its claims; it may ship with no `seoTitle` and a distribution/contact plan instead. Scores compare honest candidates; they do not predict traffic.

## Worked examples from the current catalog

### 1. Hybrid engineering doorway — `You Don’t Always Need Codemap`

Content inventory: entities = Codemap, ghx, Aider repo maps, Repomix, Gitingest; problem = code-context advice starts after a repository is already chosen/cloned; mechanisms = explore, search, map, pack, read; bounded outcome = decide whether a repository deserves to be read. Non-promise = universal winner or full setup tutorial.

Primary job: an agentic engineer comparing pre-clone and post-clone context operations needs to choose the right class of tool. Secondary phrases: `agent code context`, `repo maps`, `repository exploration`, `code search`, `repo packing`.

The current package already demonstrates the correct split: literary H1, concrete `seoTitle`, description naming all five tools and their distinct jobs, and a visible orientation sentence that immediately confirms the cast and decision moment. A weaker comparison-slug title would imply a universal tool ranking the article does not provide.

### 2. Narrow identity doorway — `Life as Background Music`

Content inventory: entity/scene = listening to *City of Stars*; problem = liking, wanting, and feeling while keeping them safely in the background; mechanism = the lived pattern the essay names; outcome = personal recognition, not an optimization method.

Package rule: keep the metaphorical H1, keep the precise existing description, and let the opening scene orient the reader. `seoTitle: How to Stop Living on Autopilot` would be rejected: it invents a self-help job and resolved outcome. Search may remain narrow; distribution should quote the precisely named feeling, and success may be a reader saying “this is me,” not broad query volume.

### 3. Technical/human hybrid — `My Evals Say It Works. I Still Don’t Reach for It.`

Content inventory: entities = ghx sidecar, 90-episode eval, 25× compression, usage logs; problem = capability evidence did not create a habit; mechanisms = setup tax, workflow fit, direct ghx use, adoption gap; outcome = a three-day usage burst followed by silence, with the product question still live.

Primary job: a builder evaluating agent-product adoption needs evidence for why passing evals may not predict habitual use. Secondary phrases: `AI agent evals`, `founder dogfooding`, `sidecar agents`, `product adoption gap`, `developer workflow`. H1 should stay. A viable cold-search candidate is `ghx Sidecar Evals: 25× Compression, No Lasting Habit`; it must remain paired with the existing description and opening so “no lasting habit” is immediately evidenced and not misread as “the eval was invalid.” A title promising “why AI evals fail” is rejected because the article explicitly trusts the eval.

## Post-publication query learning loop

### Before publication/change

- Save the package, promise ledger, evidence sources, Search Console baseline/export if the URL exists, and the chosen window.
- Record the exact change date with an annotation. Request indexing when useful, then stop editing while Google recrawls.
- Default policy recommendation for this low-volume publication: inspect after 28 complete post-recrawl days and again at 90 days. This is an operating cadence, not an evidence-backed universal threshold; extend when impressions are sparse.

### Observe

Export Search Console queries filtered to the exact page and fixed search type/country/device when relevant. Save clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, date range, and shown title/snippet examples for diagnostic queries. Bucket queries manually:

- primary-fit;
- secondary/adjacent-fit;
- wrong-format (docs/tutorial/comparison/download when article is not one);
- wrong-interpretation/entity ambiguity;
- branded/named-work;
- unknown/insufficient context.

Keep direct reader replies, corrections, community referrals, attributable distribution, and earned links beside query data. Search Console is partial and lagged; do not backfill hidden queries with guesses.

### Diagnose by acquisition stage

- **Doorway optimization:** good-fit impressions exist but cold readers do not click, especially at useful positions. Inspect the actual query-specific title/snippet and narrow one package element. CTR is diagnostic only within comparable query/position/device contexts.
- **Content correction:** the page promise is true but a factual/technical claim is wrong or stale. Correct content first; metadata follows.
- **Content/intent gap:** observed queries ask for material absent from the article. Do not stuff it in or overpromise. Write a genuinely warranted new article/section only if it has its own living center and evidence.
- **Distribution:** little/no exposure or good-fit search demand is absent. Share the existing truthful promise in the communities where the relevant people already are; measure attributable referrals/contact separately from search. Distribution creates chances for contact, not guaranteed traffic.
- **Link earning:** a specific asset (dataset, benchmark method, diagram, decision table, reproducible failure, coined frame) is cited or linked by others. Measure contextual referring links and reuse, not pageviews. Do not manufacture an asset only to satisfy SEO.
- **Reader contact:** replies, corrections, disagreements, implementations, citations, or “this named my experience” messages. This may be the primary success signal for narrow identity pieces.
- **No action:** data is sparse, mixed, position-driven, preliminary, or already shows an honest fit.

### Change and learn

Change one coherent hypothesis at a time, annotate it, allow recrawl, and wait through the declared window. Compare equivalent periods when possible. A before/after movement is an association, not causal proof on a single page. Record `observed`, `inferred`, and `unknown` separately. Update `lastModified` only when the served page changes materially under repository rules; do not show an updated reader date for metadata-only work. Promote a lesson into the shared skill only after recurrence across articles or strong external evidence.

## Durable versus volatile rules

### Durable defaults

- Content truth and the living center precede query selection.
- One clear primary doorway is easier to verify than a bundle of intents.
- A title/link makes a promise the page must fulfill immediately.
- Unique, descriptive, accurate titles/descriptions help cold readers; Google may rewrite both.
- Query interpretation can be plural; exact keyword matching is unnecessary; stuffing damages clarity.
- Headings are navigation and semantic structure, not insertion slots.
- Meta keywords are ignored by Google.
- Search Console data is partial, lagged, aggregated, and non-causal.
- Search exposure, distribution, earned links, and human contact are different stages and require different evidence.

### Volatile, date and re-audit

- Exact visible character ranges, SERP layouts, rewrite behavior/frequency, rich-result features.
- Search Console UI, dimensions, filters, retention/export limits, anonymization behavior.
- Dominant query meanings, tool/version vocabulary, query/result types, Trends/autocomplete output.
- AI-search eligibility/selection/use/citation/referral behavior.
- Platform distribution mechanics and audience availability.
- Any CTR uplift, title formula, “power word,” or A/B winner from another site.

Skill rule: date every volatile claim and re-check the primary source at least every six months or when observed behavior conflicts. Do not turn character counts, keyword counts, or another site’s A/B winner into hard acceptance gates.

## Concrete skill implications

1. The skill should route only after `shape-article`; its first output is a protected-content inventory, not keywords.
2. It needs three explicit portfolio modes—growth, identity, hybrid—and must allow `seoTitle: none`.
3. It should require one primary doorway card, 2–5 secondary semantic phrases, a promise ledger, and the weighted rubric with hard vetoes.
4. It should teach metadata surfaces as a coherent split: H1/OG for the clicked reader; optional seoTitle/description for cold search; visible standfirst/orientation for arrival confirmation; explicit `alternativeHeadline`/tags for semantic labeling, not ranking magic.
5. It should ban keyword density, hidden meta-keyword work, comparison slugs unsupported by content, FAQ accretion, and title formulas promoted from isolated tests.
6. It should preserve the repository’s `series` + one contextual prose-link pattern and require a real editorial relationship before cross-linking.
7. Its learning loop must classify action as correction, doorway change, distribution, link earning, new content, or no action, with search and reader-contact evidence kept separate.
8. It should include the catalog examples above so agents can see that “do nothing to the H1” and “no seoTitle” are valid successful decisions.

## Unknowns and evidence limits

- No page-filtered gkoreli.com Search Console export was available in this assignment, so actual query demand and CTR for the examples remain unverified.
- There is no controlled title experiment for this single, low-volume personal publication. SearchPilot’s multi-page commercial tests establish context sensitivity, not expected uplift here.
- Google does not expose all queries or the exact causal contribution of title, snippet, content, links, competition, and personalization.
- Whether `.post-lede` and `.post-orient` should converge is an implementation/editorial decision outside this research; the skill should require at most one arrival-confirmation sentence, not a new component.
- The project’s `alternativeHeadline` and JSON-LD keywords are legitimate semantic fields, but no evidence reviewed here supports treating them as traffic levers.
- Exact measurement windows must lengthen when impressions are sparse; 28/90 days is a proposed cadence, not a guarantee.
