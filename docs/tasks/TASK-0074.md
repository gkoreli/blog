# TASK-0066 — Publication strategy for reader growth, signature, and bridge articles

## Decision

Use three first-class publication roles: **reader-growth**, **signature**, and **bridge**. A role names the job an already-shaped article can do for the publication. It does not choose the article's living center, governing form, tense, value, quality, or publication priority. Run `shape-article` first. Then infer the role from the actual article and design honest external doorways around it.

There are no quotas. A role model is useful only if it lets gkoreli.com pursue two legitimate aims at once:

1. help new readers find useful, specific work; and
2. make Goga's distinct voice, judgment, projects, ambitions, and changing thought visible.

Broad reach without signature produces a replaceable technical publication. Signature without any legible entry points may produce a meaningful private room that few right readers find. Bridge pieces can connect the two, but they are not superior and should not become a mandatory house form.

**Central rule:** broaden the doorway only as far as the article itself can honestly receive the reader. Never broaden the body to justify a search phrase, and never cryptically package a concrete article merely to look literary.

## The role model

### 1. Reader-growth article

**Publication job:** give a cold reader a clear way into an owned problem, mechanism, decision, or artifact that matters beyond the existing audience. “Broad” means a wider *fit set*, not a generic topic. A Linux signal-stack investigation may be broad within systems engineering because many implementers share the failure; an article about “AI” may be narrow if only one project's internal context makes it intelligible.

**Center test:** a stranger can state the practical question or tension before knowing Goga, and the article's owned evidence gives that stranger a useful answer, decision rule, sharper question, or bounded verdict.

**Identity contribution:** demonstrate what Goga builds, how he reasons, what evidence he trusts, and where his position differs. This is not a generic tutorial lane. Direct experience and a signed point of view keep it part of the same publication.

**Natural assets:** reproducible evidence, an architecture diagram, code, a benchmark with method, a decision table, an audit method, a failure taxonomy, or a reusable distinction. These are link-worthy when they are part of the article's real work. Do not bolt on a framework or checklist merely to manufacture backlinks.

### 2. Signature article

**Publication job:** make Goga's voice, thinking, vision, identity, or live state more legible to readers who care about the person and body of work, even when the likely audience is narrow. Its value is depth, recognition, and contact, not its ability to satisfy a large query class.

**Center test:** removing the first-person position, metaphor, unresolved contradiction, risk, or vision would remove the reason the article exists. Concrete handles may provide context, but they do not exhaust the piece's promise.

**Identity contribution:** give the publication claims, language, desires, and contradictions that no corporate engineering blog could publish. A precise feeling or public ambition may be the durable asset. It can be quoted, answered, or carried into another reader's life without being converted into a search tutorial.

**Discoverability posture:** voice may beat legibility at the H1 when the title is load-bearing. For exposed essays, `personal-essays` is stricter: no `seoTitle` gymnastics. A plain, accurate description can orient a cold reader. Sometimes the honest choice is a small doorway.

### 3. Bridge article

**Publication job:** let a concrete problem or artifact carry a cold reader into Goga's judgment, live stake, or project vision, or let a personal stake reveal a transferable engineering problem. The practical and personal centers are inseparable rather than two half-articles joined together.

**Center test:** the article would be materially weaker both as a de-personalized guide and as an essay with the implementation removed. The reader needs the mechanism and the stake.

**Identity contribution:** show why this person notices and cares about this technical problem. Bridge pieces often make the publication's full promise easiest to see, but they must arise from the material. They are not a compromise quota between “SEO posts” and “real writing.”

**Natural assets:** an owned case study plus a reusable distinction; a measured result plus the unresolved adoption problem; an architecture decision plus the ambition it serves. Distribution can use a practical hook in a technical venue and a live-stake hook in a relationship-rich venue, while both links resolve to the same honest article.

## Role decision tree

Run this only after the living center and governing form are fixed.

1. **Write the article's one-sentence truth.** What is it actually about now? Name the artifact, problem, stake, and evidence boundary. If this cannot be stated, return to `shape-article`; positioning cannot repair an unshaped article.
2. **Write the cold-reader promise.** What can a stranger receive without knowing Goga's history? Do not write a larger promise than the body fulfills.
3. **Could the article keep its reason for existing if the authorial stake were removed?**
   - Yes: candidate **reader-growth** article. Preserve the author's standing and judgment, but let the shared problem lead the doorway.
   - No: continue.
4. **Could the article keep its reason for existing if the technical mechanism, named project, or owned evidence were removed?**
   - Yes: candidate **signature** article. Let voice and living center govern the doorway; use context only to prevent accidental opacity.
   - No: candidate **bridge** article. The practical mechanism and personal stake must both remain visible.
5. **Can a concrete noun or problem phrase enter the public H1 without changing the article's emotional temperature, claim, or form?**
   - Yes: use an externally legible H1. Do not create a metadata split out of habit.
   - No: keep the voice-bearing H1. Consider a semantically aligned `seoTitle` for a voice-forward engineering or inquiry piece. For an exposed essay, normally omit it and let the description orient.
6. **Would the proposed handle attract a reader whose task the article does not perform?**
   - Yes: reject it. This is intent laundering, even if the term appears in the body.
   - No: keep it and verify that the first 100 words give the cold reader enough context to recognize the promised article.
7. **Does the article directly continue, correct, deepen, or bound another post?**
   - Yes: use a hard prose link at the relevant sentence. Add `series` metadata only for a deliberate reading sequence.
   - No: do not add “related” links merely because tags overlap.
8. **Choose distribution by reader fit and available context.** Search and technical aggregators suit some reader-growth articles; subscribers and personal networks suit many signature articles; bridge articles may earn different truthful hooks per channel. Channel choice is an editorial hypothesis, never a traffic forecast.

## When the public H1 should carry external handles

Use a concrete, externally legible H1 when all of these are true:

- the named problem, artifact, tool, or mechanism belongs to the living center rather than serving as bait;
- a cold reader can understand the article's promise from that wording;
- the wording still sounds like Goga and preserves the article's actual level of certainty;
- the body substantially fulfills the implied intent; and
- adding the handle makes the title more precise rather than more generic.

This is common for reader-growth articles and possible for any role. “How I Built First-Party Analytics for a Personal Blog” is not less authored because it is concrete. The first person, owned system, architecture, and evidence can carry identity. A literary H1 is not a badge of seriousness.

Use only `seoTitle` and/or description for concrete handles when the H1's exact sentence does editorial work that the handles would damage: it names a live contradiction, carries a metaphor earned by the body, preserves an exposed confession, or speaks in the author's unmistakable arguing voice. This split is most useful for voice-forward engineering inquiries and bridge pieces. The `seoTitle` must share the H1's substance and describe the page, because Google may derive title links from the HTML title, visible H1, `og:title`, page text, and incoming anchors. A title split is a preference signal, not control.

Let discoverability yield to voice when:

- the governing form is an exposed essay and `personal-essays` protects a plain doorway;
- a keyworded H1 would falsely resolve a live question into a guide or verdict;
- concrete product names would make a human piece look like product documentation;
- a search phrase attracts docs/reference/comparison intent the article does not satisfy;
- the author's exact phrase is itself the publication value; or
- the only reason to add a handle is estimated volume, not content fit.

Yielding to voice does not mean withholding context. The description, first paragraph, tags, explicit `alternativeHeadline` where appropriate, and a natural predecessor link can orient without flattening the H1.

For non-exposed inquiry or vision, use only a plain, faithful semantic label when it helps the right cold reader. No “keyword gymnastics.” | Often useful: name the project/topic and practical problem while retaining semantic overlap with H1. |
| Description | Name the system/problem, evidence, and bounded reader value. A pitch is allowed only in the sense of an accurate page summary. | State plainly what the piece is living through or thinking about. Add a project/topic only when it materially grounds the piece. Never turn the thesis into a generic self-help promise. | Name both sides: artifact/evidence and live stake/uncertainty. Do not advertise a settled answer the ending refuses. |

Add only when it honestly labels a named concept or project; do not create machine-facing pseudo-prose. | Useful for explicit artifact + problem labeling when the H1 is voiced. |

The site's surface contract remains: `<title>` serves the cold search result; H1 and `og:title` carry the real title; JSON-LD `headline` follows the H1; `alternativeHeadline` is explicit; tags feed JSON-LD keywords; publish dates do not change for metadata edits; `lastModified` changes only when the served page changes materially.

## Portfolio identity and growth work together

Reader-growth articles create **first contact** around problems people already know they have. Signature articles create **recognition and memory**: why this publication and person are worth returning to. Bridge articles create **continuity between utility and identity**. Internal links and deliberate series trails let those jobs reinforce each other without making any single article perform all three.

A healthy path may look like this, but it is not a required funnel:

- a reader finds an analytics architecture article because the title names Cloudflare Workers and D1;
- a contextual link takes them to the live measurement doubt that caused the rebuild;
- a series trail later shows how the evidence changed;
- a reply, correction, subscription, citation, or later return establishes contact.

The reverse path is equally valid: a reader recognizes himself in a signature essay, then follows a project link into the engineering work. Portfolio identity does not live only in narrow pieces. Reader-growth articles should retain first person, dead ends, direct evidence, and signed judgment. Growth does not live only in broad pieces. A precise signature essay may travel widely because readers feel seen, but that possibility is not a reason to rewrite it around a forecast.

## Time and form, without rigidity

Time is evidence and position, not a role classifier.

| Time/form condition | Possible role | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Past-tense retrospective with earned lessons | Reader-growth, signature, or bridge | A reusable decision can widen the doorway; a changed identity may remain the center; both can coexist when inseparable. |
| Present-tense field note or active failure | Any role | It can serve a broad reader if the current mechanism is evidenced and useful. Do not wait for resolution merely to make it searchable. |
| Exposed essay | Usually signature, sometimes broadly resonant | Role does not license search packaging. `personal-essays` controls voice, ending, and the no-gymnastics doorway. |
| Future-facing project vision | Signature or bridge; sometimes reader-growth | A vision is legible when its architecture and intended change are concrete. Do not pretend future outcomes already exist. |
| Engineering argument/decision | Often reader-growth or bridge | The H1 may be concrete. Keep the authorial stake when it explains the decision and does not fabricate a wound. |
| Inquiry | Signature or bridge; sometimes reader-growth | A strong question can be useful without a verdict. Metadata must not advertise “how to solve” what the article leaves open. |
| Later continuation | May change role from the predecessor | New evidence can support a reader-growth verdict even if the first post was a live bridge; a later post may become more personal instead. Each piece earns its own role. |

Do not map past = broad, present = personal, or future = vision-only. Do not require a sequence in which a narrow wound earns permission only by producing a later useful guide.

## Portfolio health signals

These are diagnostic signals, not scorecards or targets. Review over a meaningful editorial window chosen before looking at the numbers. Persistent absence prompts a question; it does not trigger a quota.

### Article-level integrity

- **Promise fit:** page-filtered queries, referral context, and reader replies describe the article that actually exists. Bad-fit impressions indicate a doorway mismatch, not automatically a body failure.
- **Voice survival:** title and metadata still sound compatible with the article when read beside its strongest passage. No role assignment made the piece generic or falsely resolved.
- **Cold-reader comprehension:** an unfamiliar reader can identify the artifact/context and evidence boundary without the article flattening its living center.
- **Contact quality:** corrections, counterexamples, implementation reports, thoughtful disagreement, personal replies, and citations show what the article enabled. Silence proves none of their opposites.

### Portfolio-level health

- **Recognizable identity:** readers can name recurring projects, questions, methods, and positions, not merely a list of high-volume topics. Reader replies and organic descriptions of the publication are stronger evidence than a tag count.
- **Useful entry points:** some articles have accurate cold-reader doorways into common problems the publication genuinely owns. Relevant search queries, qualified referrals, and implementer contact are evidence. Raw impressions are not.
- **Continuity:** predecessor/sequel/correction relationships use contextual prose links, and true reading sequences use `series`. The site shows thought changing across time instead of isolated posts sharing tags.
- **Cross-role movement:** attributable navigation or reader comments sometimes show that a utility entry point led to voice/vision work, or vice versa. Do not demand a funnel or identify individuals to prove it.
- **Link earning:** outside authors cite a method, dataset, model, phrase, or evidence artifact because it helped their work. A crawler request or supplied-source use does not prove organic discovery, selection, citation, or referral.
- **Editorial range:** over time, the publication still has permission to publish earned lessons, live uncertainty, project architecture, ambition, and personal vision. The signal is freedom and truthfulness, not a balanced count.
- **No orphaned identity:** if reader-growth posts attract contact but readers cannot tell what Goga stands for, inspect voice and cross-references. If signature work is strong but consistently lacks any honest doorway or context for unfamiliar readers, inspect metadata and orientation. Neither diagnosis sets a production quota.

### Keep four outcomes separate

1. **Doorway optimization** changes how an existing article is represented: H1, `seoTitle`, description, standfirst/orientation, headings, tags, and internal anchors. Evidence includes page-filtered query fit, search position, title/snippet rendering, and qualified clicks. It cannot guarantee traffic or contact.
2. **Distribution** puts the article before a chosen group: X, LinkedIn, HN, Reddit, dev.to, RSS, or direct outreach. Evidence includes a recorded share, attributable referral, and discussion in that venue. Distribution is not discoverability and does not prove link earning.
3. **Link earning** is another publisher choosing to reference the work. Evidence is the actual backlink/citation and the asset cited. It is not guaranteed by adding tables, original data, or “quotable” prose.
4. **Reader contact** is a reply, correction, subscription, implementation report, invitation, sustained return, or other direct consequence involving a reader. Pageviews, impressions, crawls, and AI-UA matches do not establish it.

Julia Evans's primary-source account supports treating conversations, help, corrections, and ongoing subscribers as more meaningful than maximizing pageviews. It also shows that publicizing through aggregators can help find interested readers while remaining distinct from the deeper success condition. This is evidence for the outcome separation, not proof that her channel mix or metrics transfer unchanged to gkoreli.com.

## Worked examples from the current publication

### Reader-growth: “How I Built First-Party Analytics for a Personal Blog”

- **Observed package:** H1 names the owned build and common problem; `seoTitle` names “Blog Analytics with Cloudflare Workers and D1”; description promises a code-backed guide to edge events, privacy, migrations, and public stats.
- **Why the H1 can be external:** the concrete subject is the living engineering work, not an imposed keyword shell. The first person and personal-blog boundary keep the standing clear.
- **Portfolio job:** offer implementers an entry point, then use the measurement-boundaries series and contextual prose links to expose the larger argument about what metrics can claim.
- **Do not infer:** the package does not forecast readers, ranking, links, or contact.

### Signature: “Life as Background Music”

- **Observed package:** literary H1; no `seoTitle`; plain description: “On realizing how easy it is to like things, want things, even feel things, and still keep them safely in the background.”
- **Why discoverability yields:** explaining the metaphor inside the H1 would reduce the phrase that carries the essay's identity. The description gives a cold reader enough truth without turning it into “how to stop emotional avoidance.”
- **Portfolio job:** create recognition and memory through precise language. Distribution should quote the actual line or premise to readers who can receive it, not manufacture a generic self-improvement promise.

### Bridge: “My Evals Say It Works. I Still Don't Reach for It.”

- **Observed package:** voice-bearing H1; `seoTitle` names the ghx sidecar, eval, and broken habit; description supplies the 90-episode eval, 25× compression, three-day burst, and silence. The ghx field-notes series supplies continuity.
- **Why the split works:** a stranger gets concrete artifact and evidence handles while the public title preserves the central contradiction. Removing the usage logs would make it unsupported personal doubt; removing the founder's failure to adopt would make it an eval report and erase the reason the result matters.
- **Portfolio job:** carry eval and agent-tool readers into Goga's harder product judgment. A technical distribution hook can lead with eval/adoption; a personal hook can lead with building something proven that the builder does not use. Neither should promise a generic eval tutorial.

### Current exposed-essay caution: “I Am Lost Right Now”

The corpus currently includes an explicit `seoTitle` for this exposed essay. The current `personal-essays` skill says exposed pieces should have no `seoTitle` gymnastics. This role model does **not** imply an automatic migration: AGENTS says not to churn metadata without page-filtered query evidence, and historical frontmatter is not itself proof of harm. For future exposed essays, default to the voice-bearing H1 plus an honest description. Revisit an existing split only for observed mismatch or a separate editorial reason.

## Explicit non-rules

- No percentage, count, cadence, or alternating schedule for the three roles.
- No requirement that every topic appear once in each role.
- No assumption that reader-growth means high traffic or signature means low traffic. Those are roles, not forecasts.
- No rule that broad articles are more valuable, more mature, more technical, or more publishable.
- No rule that narrow articles must be exposed, wounded, present-tense, or written without evidence.
- No rule that bridge articles are the ideal form or that every engineering article needs a personal wound.
- No automatic `seoTitle` split. Most concrete H1s should do both jobs.
- No automatic keyword removal from literary titles. Concrete nouns belong when they strengthen the real sentence.
- No exact-match keyword headings, FAQ blocks, comparison slugs, companion SEO pages, or body expansion unless the article independently needs them.
- No rewriting an unresolved inquiry as a solution, guide, verdict, or retrospective to improve discoverability.
- No turning an exposed essay into self-help through its description.
- No treating tags or JSON-LD keywords as ranking magic.
- No deriving `alternativeHeadline` from `seoTitle`; set it explicitly or omit it.
- No “related posts” blocks based only on shared tags. Use deliberate series and contextual prose links.
- No publishing on every channel. Choose places where the article's right readers and norms fit.
- No changing H1, metadata, body, and distribution together in response to daily noise. Diagnose the stage first and wait for the declared observation window.
- No promising traffic, backlinks, citation, subscription, or contact. Optimize an honest doorway, distribute deliberately, make real assets referenceable, and observe what happens.
- No interpreting crawls, impressions, or AI-UA requests as readers, use, citations, or outcomes.
- No date bump for metadata-only work and no visible “updated” date without a material reader-facing change.
- No portfolio health conclusion from one quiet article or one viral article.

### Synthesis/inference

- The three roles are an editorial synthesis, not an externally validated taxonomy. Their value is preserving distinct publication jobs while preventing one optimization logic from governing every article.
- A portfolio can grow identity and reach through cross-role continuity, but there is no evidence for a universal mix or sequence.
- Concrete reader-growth articles can strengthen identity when they retain owned evidence and judgment; signature pieces can create reach when they precisely name a shared experience. Neither outcome can be forecast from role.

## Unknowns

- No page-filtered Search Console query export was provided for the current corpus, so this artifact does not judge whether any existing metadata package is winning or losing the right intent.
- No reader interviews or reply corpus establishes how current readers describe gkoreli.com's identity.
- No measurement shows whether readers move between roles through current series trails or prose links.
- No channel is predictably right for a future article; venue norms, timing, audience fit, and the article itself must be checked at publication.
- No evidence supports an optimal role ratio, publishing frequency, title formula, or traffic outcome.
- Search engines may rewrite titles and snippets; an honest metadata split influences but does not control the external result.
- Link-worthy assets may earn no links, and a signature phrase may travel without an attributable backlink.

## Concrete implications for `article-discovery-positioning`

1. **Hard boundary:** start from the shaped article, not a market opportunity. Accept the living center, governing form, protected passages, evidence boundary, and honest ending as inputs. Never choose or flatten them.
2. **Require a role declaration:** `reader-growth`, `signature`, or `bridge`, followed by two sentences of evidence from the article. Include “role is not a forecast.” Allow uncertainty; do not force a false hybrid.
3. **Require a cold-reader contract:** one promise, one likely intent, and explicit non-promises. Reject a handle if the body does not fulfill the implied task.
4. **Add an H1 decision:** prefer a single concrete H1 when it remains true to the center. Split H1/`seoTitle` only when the H1 is load-bearing and the semantic label remains faithful. Add an exposed-essay veto against `seoTitle` gymnastics.
5. **Emit a surface-by-surface metadata package:** H1/`og:title`, optional `seoTitle`, description, optional `alternativeHeadline`, tags, first-100-word anchor or explicit reason not to add one, and heading guidance. Never output keywords as a detached volume list.
6. **Protect form in headings:** engineering navigation may align with reader questions; inquiry and essay headings follow movement; exposed essays may stay sparse. The skill may add orientation, never a false answer.
7. **Design continuity:** identify predecessor, sequel, correction, deeper dive, or boundary relationships. Recommend `series` only for a deliberate sequence and one contextual root-relative prose link per real relationship.
8. **Separate four plans:** doorway optimization, distribution, link earning, and reader contact. For each, name the action, evidence that would show it happened, and what it still cannot prove.
9. **Make distribution channel-specific but non-predictive:** one truthful hook per chosen venue, canonical source first, and a reason the readers/norms fit. Never promise reach or require every channel.
10. **Include a role-specific asset check:** identify an existing link-worthy asset or state that none is needed. Never invent data, a framework, or a checklist solely for links.
11. **Use a portfolio diagnostic, not quotas:** ask whether current publishing goals lack honest entry points, signature depth, or continuity. Persistent absence may suggest an editorial opportunity; it never schedules the next article.
12. **Post-publication loop:** inspect page-filtered query fit, actual title/snippet, attributable referrals, real backlinks/citations, and reader contact after a declared window. Choose one of: content correction, doorway change, distribution change, new article/experiment, or no action. Do not change multiple stages at once without a reason.
13. **Keep evidence states explicit:** observations, external evidence, inference, and unknowns. Traffic cannot be guaranteed; crawler access is not discovery; retrieval is not use; use is not citation; a click is not contact.

The skill's durable question should be: **Given the article that honestly exists, which right readers could receive it, what truthful words and paths would let them recognize it, and which parts of the publication should remain narrow because that narrowness is the point?**
