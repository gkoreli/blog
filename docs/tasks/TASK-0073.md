# TASK-0065 — Internal linking, series continuity, source links, link-worthy assets, and ethical backlink earning

## Assignment and boundary

This artifact designs the linking and distribution component of `article-discovery-positioning` for a personal engineering publication. It does **not** choose the article's living center, form, voice, argument, or ending. `shape-article` governs form and protected material; `personal-essays` governs voice and exposed prose; `blog-writing` governs structure and sourcing; `shareable-engineering` supplies existing trust/share evidence. The new skill should own the translation from the article's actual relationships and original material into honest links, stable citation targets, and publication routes.

Traffic cannot be guaranteed. Keep four outcomes separate:

1. **Doorway optimization:** on-site navigation, crawlable contextual links, anchors, canonical metadata, and source legibility. This improves eligibility and comprehension; it does not prove ranking, referral, or reading.
2. **Distribution:** putting the canonical article in front of a plausibly interested audience. A post or submission proves exposure only to the extent the channel reports it.
3. **Link earning:** an unaffiliated editor voluntarily chooses the article or asset because it helps their readers. A backlink proves that editorial choice, not that anyone clicked or read.
4. **Reader contact:** attributable visits, replies, corrections, citations, subscriptions, implementations, and conversations. This is the outcome closest to the publication's purpose.

Never report a crawler request, social impression, submission, backlink, or pageview as interchangeable evidence.

## Relationship taxonomy

A link must have a named relationship. If the editor cannot name one, do not add it.

### Internal editorial relationships

| Relationship | Direction | Reader job | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Predecessor / assumed state** | New → old | Supplies origin or prior state the new piece assumes | Backward prose link near the first assumption; summarize enough that the new article still stands alone |
| **Continuation / outcome** | Old → new and new → old | Shows what happened after a declared open state | Add a dated forward sentence to the older post at the former open loop; add a backward sentence to the continuation |
| **Correction / supersession** | Both ways | Prevents a reader from treating obsolete reasoning as current | Visible note adjacent to the affected claim (or at top if pervasive), date it, link both directions, and preserve what was believed then rather than silently rewriting history |
| **Deep dive / expansion** | Overview → detail; detail → overview when orientation helps | Lets the current article stay focused while offering mechanism or implementation depth | Explain the minimum necessary in place, then link; never outsource a load-bearing premise |
| **Boundary companion** | Usually both ways | Shows the adjacent case deliberately excluded or where the rule stops | Place beside the scope boundary, with anchor text naming the excluded case |
| **Evidence dependency** | Claim → reproduction, benchmark, data, code, or methodology | Lets a reader inspect the evidence behind a specific claim | Claim-adjacent link to a stable artifact/version; name evidence state and limitations |
| **Dialogue / changed mind** | Article → the proposition it answers; often reciprocal internally | Shows agreement, disagreement, or evolution of position | Link beside the proposition, state the relationship honestly, and do not flatten disagreement into generic relatedness |
| **Deliberate sequence** | Series trail plus direct prose links where earned | Provides an ordered reading path | Use existing `series { id, title, order }`; keep stable `id`; real article titles; duplicate order must fail; the automatic trail does not replace a direct contextual relationship |
| **Curated topic/project hub** | Hub ↔ selected pieces | Gives non-linear entry paths by reader need rather than chronology | Create/update only after a durable cluster has at least three substantial pieces with distinct reader jobs and an organizing question; annotate `start here`, current state, decisions, evidence, and continuations; do not auto-dump every tag match |
| **Tag overlap only** | None | No demonstrated reader need | Not link-eligible. Tags already provide weak topical grouping |

The “three pieces” hub threshold is an editorial maintenance guardrail, not a ranking claim. A two-part linear sequence is a series, not a hub. A hub is justified only when ordering alone no longer tells a reader where to go.

### External source relationships

- **Origin/attribution:** the person or document that introduced the term, claim, project, or idea. Link to the originator, not an aggregator.
- **Claim evidence:** the study, primary documentation, repository state, dataset, or first-party observation that supports a sentence. Link at that sentence.
- **Implementation evidence:** exact code, issue, commit, release, or reproduction. Use commit/release permalinks for version-specific claims.
- **Counterevidence/limit:** evidence that narrows or disputes the article's position. Give it the same directness as supporting evidence.
- **Further exploration:** genuinely useful depth not required to accept the current claim. Label the reader job; never disguise filler as a source.
- **People/work credit:** name the creator and link their original work. A mention is not outreach inventory.

A glossary/reference table can mirror and date sources for scanning, but it must not be the only place a reader can connect a contested claim to its evidence.

## Placement and anchor rules

1. **Link at the moment of need.** Put a source beside its claim, a predecessor beside the first assumed state, a boundary companion beside the boundary, and a continuation beside the former open loop. Do not collect context links in a generic “related posts” block.
2. **Backward links orient; forward links preserve memory.** A new article is not complete until the relevant older article is checked for the earned forward link. Do not change the older article's original tense or pretend the later result was known. Use a dated update sentence where needed.
3. **One contextual link per relationship.** Do not repeat the same destination throughout the body. The deliberate automatic series trail is the allowed complementary navigation mechanism.
4. **Use root-relative canonical internal URLs** (`/slug`) and the preferred canonical external URL. Never link internally to a duplicate/cross-posted variant.
5. **Write descriptive anchors that predict the destination.** Prefer the article title or a concise concept/artifact phrase: “the ghx origin story and `read --map` decision,” not “click here,” “read more,” or “this article.” Keep anchors natural, concise, and relevant to both pages.
6. **No anchor optimization theater.** Do not force exact-match keywords, rotate anchors mechanically, chain adjacent links, or inflate the linked phrase. The whole sentence should explain why the destination helps.
7. **Keep the current page sufficient.** Links are escape hatches for optional history, evidence, or depth, not substitutes for definitions, premises, safety constraints, or the current verdict.
8. **Prefer exact, stable source targets.** Link to the exact documentation section, paper, release, issue, or commit-pinned line range used. For a changing page, record author/title/publication date and access date in the research record. Do not mirror copyrighted sources merely to avoid link rot.
9. **Expose corrections.** If the destination no longer supports the claim, correct or qualify the claim; do not merely swap in a convenient source. Apply `lastModified` only under the existing material-change rules.
10. **Protect form and voice.** Do not interrupt an exposed passage with editorial plumbing. In an exposed essay, the agent may recommend a metadata trail or flag the natural location, but Goga must author any new prose sentence. Narrow identity pieces need no artificial project graph.
11. **No footer recommendation carousel.** A finite, explicit series trail or curated hub is navigation; automated same-tag cards are related-post spam.
12. **Accessibility is part of honesty.** The link's purpose must be clear from its text or immediate sentence/paragraph, destinations with the same purpose should be described consistently, and image-only links need useful alt text.

## Source-link protocol

For every factual or attributed link, record: (a) claim served; (b) relationship type; (c) original/primary source preference; (d) author/organization; (e) source date/version; (f) exact target; (g) access date when the page is mutable; (h) evidence limit/counterevidence.

Priority: original author/project/repository → primary product or standards documentation → peer-reviewed/original research → authoritative first-hand practitioner/company engineering account → reputable secondary synthesis. Free reader access remains the publication rule. Link directly; never cite a search-result page, summary farm, or aggregator when the original exists.

For code/data claims:

- pin exact evidence to commit SHA, release tag, DOI, or immutable artifact;
- keep a separate current-project link only when the reader also needs the maintained latest state; label “version inspected” versus “current project”;
- include commands/environment/methodology and raw outputs or machine-readable data where the claim depends on them;
- publish limitations and evidence that would change the conclusion;
- include a reuse license for original downloadable assets;
- never claim that a checksum, permalink, or DOI validates correctness—each only stabilizes identity/integrity.

## Link-worthy original asset checklist

An original asset is optional. Create/extract one only when the article's actual work already contains something another writer, maintainer, or reader would rationally cite or reuse. Do not manufacture a generic infographic or thin “statistics” page for backlinks.

A link-worthy asset should pass all applicable checks:

- **Solves a referencing job:** saves another person from reproducing analysis, explaining a mechanism, finding scattered primary sources, or demonstrating a failure.
- **Original and article-native:** derives from Goga's implementation, observation, synthesis, taxonomy, or experiment—not a decorated summary of other people's work.
- **Narrow claim:** title/caption says exactly what it establishes and what it does not.
- **Inspectable:** methodology, inputs, versions/dates, environment, sample, exclusions, and failed attempts are visible.
- **Reproducible where feasible:** commands/code and raw or machine-readable outputs are available; non-reproducible first-party observations are labeled as such.
- **Stable target:** its URL will survive article edits; exact versions use commit/release/DOI links; redirects are maintained if slugs move.
- **Self-contained citation surface:** author, title, date/version, short summary, and canonical URL are visible without requiring a social thread for context.
- **Reusable:** SVG/PNG/CSV/JSON/code is available when useful, with alt text/schema and an explicit license; quotations and third-party data respect rights and attribution.
- **Shows limits and counterexamples:** no visual or table overstates sample size, causality, generality, or freshness.
- **Useful without a backlink:** the asset earns its place in the article even if nobody links to it.

Strong candidates for this publication: minimal runnable reproductions; commit-pinned code walkthroughs; benchmark reports with raw data/scripts; decision matrices grounded in an actual cutover; architecture/mechanism diagrams with accessible source files; source timelines/provenance ledgers; reusable query/schema definitions; small original datasets with methodology; taxonomies with examples and edge cases; interactive demonstrations that materially improve understanding.

Weak candidates: generic “ultimate guides,” stock diagrams, uncited number roundups, AI-generated infographics, novelty calculators, asset pages that duplicate the article, and research shaped around what is presumed to attract links.

## Distribution and ethical outreach boundary

### Allowed sequence

1. Publish on `gkoreli.com` first; verify the canonical URL and stable asset targets.
2. Choose channels from the article's actual reader and portfolio role. Broad reader-growth pieces may use broader communities; narrow voice/thinking/vision pieces may go only to existing readers or the few people named by the question; hybrids can pair one broad technical hook with one identity-native note. Broadness is not a quality ranking.
3. Write a channel-native hook that names the concrete problem, evidence, or live question. Do not paste the same promotional copy everywhere.
4. Cross-post only where useful, with `canonical_url` pointing to gkoreli.com; use an excerpt when a full duplicate would fragment conversation.
5. Submit to communities only when the piece fits their rules and current conversation. Disclose authorship where the platform expects it. On HN, preserve the original non-linkbait title, use the site for curiosity rather than primarily promotion, and never solicit votes/comments/submissions.
6. Notify a cited creator or affected maintainer only when there is a real relationship: thank/credit them, state the exact passage or artifact built from their work, invite correction, and do not ask for a link.
7. Send a direct note to an adjacent practitioner/editor only when the article or asset clearly helps a problem they or their readers already have. Identify the exact useful section, disclose that it is yours, make ignoring easy, and stop after one unanswered note. Offer evidence or correction, never a quid pro quo.
8. Use permission-based newsletter/RSS and existing social accounts for readers who chose the relationship. Record the specific channel/hook/date so referrals can be interpreted.
9. If reach is paid, label the placement and require `rel=\"sponsored\"` (or `nofollow`) on paid links. Paid reach is distribution, not earned authority.

### Prohibited

- buying links, paying for posts that pass ranking credit, gifts/services conditioned on a link;
- reciprocal-link deals, private link rings, partner/resource pages made for cross-linking;
- automated or mass cold outreach, scraped email lists, repeated follow-ups, fake familiarity;
- optimized-anchor guest posts/press releases, low-quality directories/bookmarks, forum/comment/profile drops;
- embedded widget/template/footer links designed to pass ranking credit;
- asking friends or communities to upvote, submit, comment, or link;
- notifying every cited person as a pretext for promotion;
- turning criticism, correction, or community participation into a backlink demand;
- creating content primarily to manipulate links or search placement.

The ethical test: **Would this message still be worth sending if links carried no ranking value, and would the recipient be glad to receive it even if they never shared it?** If no, do not send.

## Post-publish audit

### Immediate release audit (same day)

- Canonical article resolves; cross-post canonical points back; internal links target root-relative canonical slugs.
- Every new link is crawlable and has a named relationship, clear anchor, and useful immediate context.
- Source targets resolve and still support the adjacent claim; exact code/data evidence is pinned to the inspected version.
- Series `id/title/order` is correct; new backward prose link exists; all earned older forward links were added; the trail renders in HTML and `.md` under the existing system.
- Asset page/file exposes author, version/date, methodology/limits, reuse terms, accessible representation, and canonical location as applicable.
- Distribution messages disclose authorship, match community rules, make no traffic promises, and contain no vote/link ask.

### Declared observation window

Define the window before reacting. Search changes generally need at least a few weeks and can take months; do not churn internal links or metadata from daily noise. Capture separately:

- page-filtered search queries, impressions, position, and clicks (doorway/search contact);
- channel-specific submissions/posts and attributable referrals (distribution);
- newly discovered editorial links, linking page, anchor/context, and whether the link is genuine/qualified (link earning);
- replies, corrections, citations, subscriptions, implementer reports, and conversations (reader contact).

A backlink with zero referral may still be a useful citation; a referral without a backlink is still distribution; neither proves reading. Pageviews alone do not establish any of these.

At the window end, choose exactly one primary action: content correction, discoverability/internal-link change, distribution change, bounded experiment, or no action. Record why. Update a shared skill only after a repeated failure or one costly preventable rule gap, per the editorial learning loop.

### Event-driven and periodic maintenance

- On every continuation: audit predecessor, forward link, backward link, series order, hub annotation, and original open-loop wording.
- On every source correction/retraction/version change: re-check affected claim, direct source target, glossary row, visible correction note, and `lastModified` materiality.
- Periodically (quarterly is a reasonable maintenance trigger, not a ranking rule): check broken/redirected external links, mutable pages that no longer support claims, branch links used for version-specific code, orphaned important articles, stale hub descriptions, and links added only because tags match.
- Preserve useful redirects when article or asset URLs move. Remove or rewrite links whose reader job disappeared; do not keep dead navigation for historical link counts.
- Archive the research record, not unauthorized copies of entire third-party works.

## Concrete implications for `article-discovery-positioning`

The skill should require a compact **link plan** after the living center and content inventory are fixed:

```text
relationship | source page/claim | destination | reader job | placement | anchor | direction | stable target | required reciprocal update
```

It should then output:

1. **Internal link map:** only named direct relationships; series/hub decision; predecessor forward-link checklist.
2. **Source map:** claim-adjacent primary links with dates/versions/limits and exact evidence targets.
3. **Asset decision:** `none` is valid; otherwise one article-native asset plus stability/reuse/reproducibility requirements.
4. **Distribution plan:** audience/channel/hook/canonical/disclosure; classify every action as distribution, not “backlink building.”
5. **Outreach list:** only real cited/affected/adjacent relationships, exact recipient value, no link request, one-note limit.
6. **Measurement plan:** predeclared window and separate evidence for doorway, distribution, earned links, and reader contact.
7. **Maintenance triggers:** continuation, correction/retraction, source drift, URL move, and periodic link-rot/orphan audit.

The skill must reject fixed internal-link quotas, same-tag related cards, universal hub creation, forced assets, mandatory outreach, and backlink/traffic promises. It should never add prose to an exposed essay; it can flag the relationship and placement for Goga or use existing metadata navigation without flattening the voice.

## Unknowns and research limits

- No credible evidence found for a universal optimal number of internal links, anchor variants, hub size, outreach messages, or posting channels; proposed counts are editorial guardrails, not ranking formulas.
- No publication-specific data yet shows which communities produce durable reader contact or voluntary editorial links for gkoreli.com. Channel choice must remain a measured hypothesis.
- Google documentation explains crawling, interpretation, canonicalization, and policy, but does not guarantee ranking, referral, citation, or traffic from these practices.
- Simon Willison and HN provide credible practitioner/community evidence, not controlled causal estimates.
- The repository supports ordered series trails; whether a dedicated curated section-hub surface should be implemented is an architecture/product decision outside this research. Do not fake hubs with generic tag dumps in the meantime.
- A durable third-party web-source preservation policy is not established. Until one exists, store bibliographic facts and exact targets, prefer primary stable URLs, use commit/release/DOI artifacts where appropriate, and avoid unauthorized mirroring.
