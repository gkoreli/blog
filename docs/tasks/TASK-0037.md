# TASK-0029 — Immediate article positioning and distribution thesis

## Editorial decision

Publish now as an **evidence-led engineering article governed by a present-tense field note, with a bounded technical argument inside it**. Let it braid three honest times: the past mistake and earned architecture lessons, the uncertainty still alive now, and the future intent behind the separate 30-day continuation. This form fits this material; it is not a house template or exposure quota. Do not erase earned expertise or settled technical conclusions, and do not manufacture vulnerability. Also do not smooth the unresolved parts into a polished postmortem, Cloudflare tutorial, or victory lap from someone who now has analytics figured out.

The publication purpose is larger than exposing pain: invite a third-person eye into Goga’s life and work so he can grow in public and readers can grow alongside him. Architecture, methods, tenets, bets, plans, vision, and attempts to change the world are first-class material beside ambiguity. Time serves the material: past for reflection and earned lessons; present for live uncertainty, conditioning, calculated judgment, and bets; future for intention, possibility, and vision. This article should use that braid because the analytics rebuild contains all three, not because every article must.

The owned event is alive: gkoreli.com had a correct counter for a later browser-beacon POST, then used stronger nouns that implied original page requests, visitors, and AI reads. The collection boundary has moved to successful HTML responses at the Worker edge, and all 2,564 legacy rows survived with their source attached. That implementation is real. The unresolved part is also real: a cleaner instrument still cannot say that anybody read, cared, learned, cited, or replied; it still cannot observe non-HTML llms.txt or Markdown requests; and the author may have spent too much effort improving analytics for a site whose product is the writing.

The current pain is the article’s living center:

- I wanted proof that the writing reaches anyone without tracking people.
- I trusted a number because its computation was correct, then discovered I had named the wrong event.
- I could not make the new model clean without deciding what to do with meaningful but incomparable history.
- I moved observation to the edge, but edge analytics is still not RUM, non-HTML request telemetry, or reader contact.
- The rewrite was necessary, yet every additional analytics improvement risks becoming another way to avoid publishing.

The 30-day window is a separate continuation. Do not wait for it, preview its result, or make this article sound as though the author already knows what the new instrument will reveal.

## Cold-reader promise

A reader who has never seen gkoreli.com should leave able to inspect one metric in their own system and ask the right question: **what exact event becomes a row, and what is the strongest noun that row can honestly support?**

The piece should give them:

1. a concrete before/after architecture from a live system;
2. a way to distinguish counter correctness from claim correctness;
3. a decision rule for beacon, edge HTML observation, RUM, and non-HTML resource observation;
4. a source-aware pattern for keeping useful history without hiding a method change; and
5. a faithful account of what a better instrument still cannot answer.

It does **not** promise a reusable analytics package, a generic Cloudflare Workers walkthrough, a universal privacy recipe, an AI-traffic detector, or post-cutover performance results.

## Signed, disagreeable, falsifiable position

> **A counter can be correct and the analytics system can still be wrong: when collection observes a browser event but the product names an original request or a read, accurate aggregation only makes the wrong claim more convincing.**

This is stronger than “name metrics carefully.” On this site, query patches and dashboard copy could not make a browser beacon observe requests that never executed it; the observation boundary had to move.

The claim is falsifiable:

- **Disprove the owned failure** by showing that an original static HTML GET independently created the old D1 row when JavaScript and POST /api/event never ran. The audited implementation shows the opposite: static pages bypassed the Worker and only the later beacon entered D1 (TASK-0012; pinned sources below).
- **Narrow the verdict** by naming the old fact accurately. If the public product had consistently said “accepted browser-beacon events,” the counter was fit for that smaller job. The article must concede this.
- **Disprove the migration result** by finding a legacy source row absent from the source-aware copy. Production evidence recorded 2,564 source rows, 2,564 copied rows, and zero missing; the source table remained untouched (TASK-0020).
- **Do not overextend the win.** The new edge metric proves an eligible HTML response was served and best-effort persistence succeeded often enough to produce a row. It does not prove reading, attention, verified identity, indexing, citation, model use, or a non-HTML resource request.

## Reader decision rule

> **Name the event before the metric: actor, request or browser action, eligibility boundary, observation point, persistence point, and strongest supportable claim. If the public noun is stronger than the event, weaken the noun or move collection. Preserve old data only with source and method attached. Add another metric only when its result can change a named action.**

| Needed fact | Correct observation surface | Honest noun | Cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| A capable client executed and sent browser code | Browser beacon | Browser/beacon event | Original request, non-JS clients, reading |
| A successful non-prefetch GET served HTML | Current edge observer | Recorded page view / served HTML page | Attention, verified identity, citation |
| Rendering, timing, errors, interactions | RUM / client observability | LCP, INP, CLS, resource timing, client error | All origin requests or no-JS traffic |
| llms.txt, page Markdown, feed, asset, or other non-HTML delivery | Separate edge/origin resource observation | Resource request / successful delivery | Selection, use, citation, human outcome |

This is the article’s new value beyond does-llms-txt-work. The prior article used the instrumentation gap to separate discovery, request, use, citation, and referral. This article owns the engineering response and its cost: moving HTML observation, correcting domain terms, repairing privacy/time semantics, preserving provenance, and confronting the fact that analytics still cannot supply the human answer the author wants.

### Armed

Readers get an event sentence, an observation-surface matrix, a source-aware migration pattern, and a stop rule they can use in an architecture review. The article should show enough concrete code and schema to make that transfer possible, not merely announce “measure the right thing.”

### Vindicated

The article confirms that discomfort with “visitor,” “human,” or “AI read” is not pedantry when the system observes a rotating identifier or a later beacon. It must also vindicate browser/RUM practitioners: their tool is not defective for failing to observe an event it was never designed to see.

### Seen

The deepest share trigger is the solo builder who wants evidence of contact and instead keeps building instruments. The useful old history makes the choice harder, not cleaner. The piece should leave both sides standing: accurate public semantics matter, and accurate page totals still cannot provide the reply, correction, link, citation, or human contact the author wants.

Editorial evidence: skill://shareable-engineering, especially “What makes an engineering article get shared,” supports specificity, one signed disagreeable claim, dead ends, loser praise, and contact over pageviews. skill://shape-article requires present-tense uncertainty and live contradiction to survive editing.

## Loser-praised requirement

Before the edge cutover wins its bounded argument, the article must praise the browser beacon:

- It correctly counted accepted browser events.
- Its 2,564 legacy rows retain useful signals about paths, referrer hosts, broad traffic classes, and daily IDs inside that method’s limits.
- Browser instrumentation is the correct surface for RUM: LCP, INP, CLS, resource timing, client failures, and interactions.
- Cloudflare Web Analytics remains a separate RUM-like layer; public D1 edge stats do not replace it.

Then state both boundaries. A browser beacon cannot observe an original request that never executes JavaScript. Edge HTML analytics cannot report browser experience, and the current eligibility policy does not record llms.txt, page Markdown, feeds, assets, redirects, errors, or API calls.

This is mandatory. Without it, “beacon vs edge” becomes a false winner-take-all comparison and the article loses trust.
