The rule "no Sec-Fetch navigate/document means automation" is only safe if real readers arriving from t.co, google.com, and in-app browsers actually send those headers. Android WebView and iOS WKWebView are documented to omit them in some cases, and X in-app readers are a main channel. After at least one daytime cycle of traffic since 2026-09-03 01:35 UTC, break down browser-UA rows by referrer host, device type, fetch-metadata presence, Accept-Language presence, and ASN. Decide whether Accept-Language absence, fetch metadata absence, or hosting ASN each stands alone as an automation verdict, and record false-positive risk for each. This blocks TASK-0102.


## Outcome (2026-09-03)

Closed on prior art instead of a site measurement. The premise that Android WebView and WKWebView omit Fetch Metadata was wrong for current versions: Android WebView has sent the headers since Chromium 76 (2019) and a 2025 production analysis (mdn/browser-compat-data #27928) shows `Sec-Fetch-Mode` from iOS WebView in the millions. caniuse reports 95.72% global support. The rule in `packages/analytics/src/readerkind.ts` version-gates absence on the claimed engine: modern claim without `Sec-Fetch-Mode` is `http-client` (reason `no-fetch-metadata`); older or unreadable claim is `legacy-browser` (reason `pre-fetch-metadata-ua`). Accept-Language absence is not a verdict on its own; hosting ASN is a verdict (`cloud-browser`), consistent with MRC and every analytics tool that uses network signals. Sources and reasoning in `packages/blog/drafts/research/readers-vs-bots/09-fetch-metadata-prior-art.md`. The 27 evidence rows that existed at the time contained no referred traffic and were not used.
