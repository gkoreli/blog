# ADR-0016.1: Analytics Purpose and Decision Loop

## Status

Accepted — 2026-08-26. Extends ADR-0016. It does not change the collection architecture or public metric contract.

## Decision

Use analytics to answer a small set of publishing and distribution questions. Every metric must support one of five actions:

1. correct content;
2. improve discoverability;
3. change distribution;
4. run a bounded experiment; or
5. do nothing.

The writing is the product. Analytics is an instrument for deciding whether the writing is reaching anyone and how readers find it. If a metric cannot change a decision, do not collect or add it.

## Why this exists

The analytics rewrite fixed an observation boundary that had started to mislead us. The old system counted browser beacon events but used stronger words such as Visitors and AI Reads. ADR-0016 repaired the event model, vocabulary, time boundaries, privacy contract, and historical continuity.

That repair was necessary, but it created a second risk: treating a more accurate instrument as a reason to keep building analytics.

The blog exists to publish engineering work worth finding. It does not exist to become an analytics product, maximize pageviews, or optimize the author's choices around a dashboard.

## Goal

Stop guessing about reach and distribution without tracking people.

The system should help answer:

- Is anyone besides the author arriving?
- Which articles attract launch traffic, sustained interest, or both?
- Which channels send readers?
- How much recorded traffic looks like ordinary browsers versus declared automation?
- Did a specific editorial, discoverability, or distribution change produce a useful signal?

The system should keep those answers public, approximate, and honest about their limits.

## What the current system can answer

| Question | Current evidence | What we can conclude | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is anyone besides the author arriving? | Daily clients, external referrer hosts, countries, and page paths | Yes. External Reddit, Google, Hacker News, X, GitHub, and ChatGPT referrers plus hundreds of daily IDs cannot be explained by author testing alone. | Daily clients are pseudonymous per-day IDs, not people. Historical owner exclusion was not reliable enough to produce an exact external-reader count. |
| Which articles attract attention? | `byPath`, UTC time series, 7d/30d/90d/All windows | Compare launch spikes, current demand, and durable traffic. | A page event does not prove reading, quality, comprehension, or approval. |
| Where do readers come from? | Referrer-host counts | Identify attributable channels and their relative contribution. | Missing referrer means unattributed, not necessarily direct. Redirects and privacy controls suppress attribution. |
| Is traffic browser, bot, or AI-related? | Browser/Bot/AI-UA classes and matched User-Agent rules | Measure the traffic classes observed by each collection method and inspect declared automation after the edge cutover. | User-Agent is sender-provided and heuristic. Browser does not mean verified human. AI-UA does not prove indexing, citation, training, or model use. |
| Did a change help? | Time series, paths, referrers, equal windows, saved worklist snapshots | Compare a declared baseline with a post-change window and decide whether the signal justifies action. | Correlation is not causation. The public UI has presets rather than experiment attribution or arbitrary-range analysis. |

## Signal hierarchy

Analytics numbers are supporting evidence. Use this order when deciding whether the blog had impact.

### Strong contact signals

- reader replies;
- corrections with specific evidence;
- newsletter subscriptions attributable to an article;
- citations and links from identifiable publications or repositories;
- reproducible reports that someone used an idea, tool, or workflow.

### Distribution and discovery signals

- page-filtered Search Console queries and clicks;
- attributable referrer hosts;
- shares with visible discussion;
- sustained page demand after the launch window.

### Context signals

- page events;
- daily clients;
- countries and Browser device mix;
- Bot and AI-UA request composition.

### Non-evidence

None of these alone proves quality, reading, agreement, comprehension, indexing, citation, or business value.

## Decision loop

For a material article or analytics change:

1. **Name the decision first.** Examples: change the search doorway, change distribution, correct a claim, write a sequel, or leave the article alone.
2. **Declare the observation window.** Do not choose the window after seeing a favorable spike.
3. **Save the baseline.** Record page, traffic class, range, totals, leading referrers, and relevant search queries in the article research directory or Analytics Worklist.
4. **Make one attributable change.** Avoid changing title, description, content, links, and distribution simultaneously when the purpose is to learn.
5. **Wait for the declared window.** Do not churn a page from daily noise.
6. **Read stronger signals first.** Replies, corrections, subscriptions, citations, and attributable links outrank page totals.
7. **Choose exactly one outcome:** content correction, discoverability change, distribution change, new experiment, or no action.
8. **Preserve the evidence.** Attach the result to the article research directory or Analytics Worklist.

## Default observation windows

Defaults are starting points; declare a different window when the channel requires it.

| Purpose | Default window | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Deployment health | 24 hours | Catch missing writes, schema errors, routing mistakes, or impossible partitions. |
| Launch distribution | 7 days | Captures immediate sharing without pretending it is durable discovery. |
| Near-term article demand | 30 days | Reduces dependence on one launch spike. |
| Durable interest | 90 days and All | Shows whether demand survives distribution. |
| Search doorway change | 28 days after confirmed recrawl | Search data lags deployment; the clock starts after the changed page is observed again. |

Do not react to daily movement unless it signals a technical failure or a traceable external event.

## How the architecture supports this goal

ADR-0016 already provides the required primitives:

- Worker-first observation records successful non-prefetch HTML responses, including non-JavaScript clients.
- `page_observations` stores path, referrer host, country, daily client ID, traffic class, matched UA rule, device class, owner status, source, and UTC time.
- `observation_source` preserves the browser-beacon versus edge-response method change.
- The original `page_views` table remains the lossless historical source archive.
- `/api/stats` applies one UTC window and traffic predicate to totals, paths, time series, referrers, countries, devices, and matched rules.
- `/stats` exposes the same contract publicly with adjacent methodology.
- Analytics Worklist `FLDR-0001` preserves research, decisions, implementation reviews, deployment evidence, and future measurements.

No new analytics architecture is required to answer the declared questions. Exact intervention comparison can use saved snapshots until repeated use proves that arbitrary ranges or annotations deserve product code.

## RUM boundary

Edge analytics is not Real User Monitoring.

Edge analytics answers:

- which eligible HTML response was served;
- when and for which path;
- the attributable referrer host;
- the coarse request geography and UA-derived class.

RUM runs in the browser and answers:

- how long rendering took;
- Core Web Vitals such as LCP, INP, and CLS;
- browser resource timing;
- client-side failures and interaction performance.

Cloudflare Web Analytics remains the separate RUM-like performance layer. The custom client-observability package records selected browser errors. Neither feeds the public D1 stats.

Do not build custom RUM until a concrete performance decision cannot be answered by Cloudflare Web Analytics, existing error reports, or a focused reproduction.

## Guardrails

- Exclude author traffic through server-visible owner controls. Browser `localStorage` cannot affect an edge request.
- Never call daily clients people or range-unique visitors.
- Never call Browser verified human.
- Never call an AI-UA match a read, citation, index, training event, or verified provider request.
- Keep legacy and edge rows source-marked even when displayed as one useful timeline.
- Keep query strings, raw IP addresses, and raw User-Agent strings out of D1.
- Do not add sessions, funnels, scroll depth, or interaction events without a named decision and deletion policy.
- Do not change editorial direction because one article won a distribution lottery.
- Do not present traffic volume as a quality score.

## Non-goals

- Advertising attribution or retargeting.
- Cross-day identity or returning-person tracking.
- Session replay, heatmaps, or behavioral profiles.
- Proving that a page was read or understood.
- Proving that an AI system indexed, cited, trained on, or used content.
- Replacing Search Console, Cloudflare Web Analytics, or direct reader contact.
- Turning `@gkoreli/analytics` into a general analytics product before another real consumer exists.

## Engineering threshold

Add analytics engineering only when all conditions hold:

1. A recurring decision cannot be answered with the existing response, D1 query, Search Console export, Cloudflare Web Analytics, or saved worklist snapshot.
2. The missing observation has a precise event definition.
3. The result can change a named action.
4. Privacy, retention, and public wording are explicit.
5. The feature is cheaper to maintain than repeating the manual analysis.

Otherwise, query the existing data or choose no action.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Analytics work stays subordinate to publishing.
- The dashboard has a falsifiable job instead of accumulating attractive metrics.
- Decisions preserve both quantitative context and stronger reader-contact evidence.
- The existing architecture is sufficient; no immediate feature backlog follows from this ADR.
- “No action” becomes an explicit successful outcome.

### Negative

- Some comparisons remain manual.
- Approximate daily identity cannot answer returning-reader questions.
- Public traffic data may invite vanity interpretation despite adjacent caveats.
- The author must record interventions and measurement windows for the data to teach anything.
