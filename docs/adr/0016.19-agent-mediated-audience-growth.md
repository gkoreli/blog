# ADR-0016.19: Agent-mediated audience growth

## Status

Accepted — October 31, 2026 UTC. Makes the audience tenet in [ADR-0016.3](0016.3-audience-composition-and-citable-articles.md) operational in growth decisions, experiments and public defaults. Amends the outcome framing in [ADR-0016.1](0016.1-analytics-purpose-and-decision-loop.md) and [ADR-0019](0019-agentic-engineering-publication-and-analytics-ambition.md).

## Owner direction

Complete message, preserved exactly:

> Btw we are not screaming chasing for the human readers people can be using their ai agents to come across us and read on behalf of them. Why dony we count that as growth? Why is this not part of our tenets please fix things

## Decision

People and agents are both audience. Discovery, retrieval, citation, synthesis and application through an agent are legitimate publication outcomes. A person need not open the browser page or subscribe directly for the publication to reach them through an agent. Direct human contact is one source of useful evidence, not the sole definition of success.

Recognize agent reach and its growth at the stage actually observed. Missing human-trigger evidence must not cancel a measured agent-access result, citation, referral or report of use. An agent may also index or retrieve autonomously; distinguish its declared role and observed action without dismissing it or inventing a person behind it.

This restores the existing composition-not-exclusion principle. The recent discovery experiment added a scope note and a workflow, while the wider analytics work produced separate substantive findings and capabilities. Neither the absence of human trials nor an unmeasured acquisition effect means those results did not happen. Blocked human/device calibration, account access or native-verifier comparisons apply to their specific claims; they are not blanket gates on publication growth work.

## Outcome contract

| Outcome | Count and interpret | Do not substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed access | Eligible served events by declared assistant, crawler, browser, automation or unresolved category, using existing exclusions | People, comprehension, or a human trigger for each request |
| Agent-mediated discovery | Captured answer mentions/citations and source selection within a declared question/service/trial sample | A sitewide or all-model citation rate |
| Distribution | Attributed external links, copies and references with first-observed date and source-version context | Unique readers, endorsement, or an inferred publication date |
| Usefulness | Supported answers, reported application, corrections and reports of use, including work completed through agents | A requirement for a human browser visit or public reply |
| Product adoption | Another operator or their agent installs and uses the analytics for an actual decision | Broad adoption established by our own deployment |

For example, 100 to 130 eligible declared-assistant events in comparable completed windows is 30% growth in those observed events. It is neither zero progress because humans are unidentified nor evidence of 30 additional people. Label supplied or controlled trials and keep them outside organic growth counts. Before interpreting a trend, check source concentration, exclusions, collection/classification history and changed question or service scope.

All eligible access is included in the overall report; value is assessed through its composition and purpose evidence. A crawler is not automatically a human-delegated assistant, and generic automation is not automatically successful use. Preserve missing, conflicting and unresolved evidence instead of relabelling it human or excluding it merely for being automated.

## Product changes

- `/stats` and an unfiltered `/api/stats` default to `all`, covering all existing disjoint request groups. Explicit category links retain their meaning. The default expands the selected cohort, not the collector, row history or exclusion policy.
- Keep `/stats/readership` and `/api/readership` as the existing focused Browser-signal report for continuity and cost. Name the page's scope explicitly, provide direct links to all-category and assistant/crawler views, and never present its trend as the whole audience's growth.
- Advance the stats cache namespace with the changed default. Cache keys still include the normalized traffic selection, so omitted and explicit `all` share a selection while explicit `browser` remains distinct.
- No new tracking, identity, retention, population relabelling or production scan is needed to implement this tenet.

## Discovery work

The observed external copy of the fetcher study is evidence that the work is distributed beyond the site. Its older claims identify a source-freshness problem worth addressing. Inspect the exact source and machine-visible version where available; distinguish the copy's availability from observed use or citation in an agent answer. A public search result is valid evidence of that sampled retrieval, even when it supplies no authenticated Google rank or human click.

The next bounded discovery work remains the external-source and reader-question audit authorized by the preceding request. The new audience tenet changes its success criteria: agent discovery and useful machine access count, and human acquisition is not its sole target. Preserve a dated question set and evidence before choosing a content or distribution intervention. External communications still need the user's authorization.

## Acceptance and continuity

[Audience-growth implementation record](../../packages/blog/drafts/research/discovery-experiment-02/00-worklist.md) owns source changes, relevant tests, publication evidence, actual discovery observations and remaining work. The earlier discovery experiment and analytics studies retain their original scopes and denominators. This ADR records the accepted direction, not a measured growth rate or retrospective claim that an agent used the new content.
