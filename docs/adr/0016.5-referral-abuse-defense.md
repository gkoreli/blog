## Context

An arbitrary `Referer` header currently buys a hostname a place in the public referral rankings. At 2026-09-06 23:25:33 UTC, the public 30-day Browsers response contained 962 views, with `uniuit.com` ranked first at 35. Those are reported header values, not verified click-throughs or people. Public names are rendered as text, so this is an analytics-promotion problem even without clickable links or HTML injection.

The saved September 4–5 investigation found 22 observations carrying this hostname, repeated homepage/article pairs, and related log candidates declaring Redroid WebView for 19 observations. These support suspected abuse, without proving the referrer's operator or motive. The 35-view current window and 22-view fixed historical window are distinct. Evidence: [artifact 12](../../packages/blog/drafts/research/readers-vs-bots/12-production-followup-2026-09-05.md) and [artifact 15](../../packages/blog/drafts/research/readers-vs-bots/15-existing-cloudflare-evidence-2026-09-06.md).

## Decision

### Public referrer names require review

Only exact hostnames in a small, versioned display catalogue can appear in the public stats API. Unreviewed hosts contribute to `otherReferrerViews`, rendered as “Other reported referrers.” They remain included in metrics unless a separate exclusion rule applies. Missing referrers remain a distinct count. A new hostname or random subdomain cannot advertise itself by sending requests, regardless of volume.

The initial catalogue names established search, social, code-hosting, and assistant services, including the reviewed common sources in the current response. Entries permit display only: a forged Google or Hacker News header remains possible. Labels say “Reported referrers”; they never imply authenticated attribution. Arbitrary subdomains are not automatically approved, and hostnames are never converted to outbound links or fetched for previews.

### Reviewed abuse rules exclude observations from public metrics

Keep a separate checked-in rule catalogue with stable rule ID, hostname, subdomain scope, reason, and evidence reference, under a dated policy version. Version every catalogue change. Initially exclude observations reporting `uniuit.com` or its subdomains, based on the repeated pattern above. Match host boundaries, normalize case and trailing DNS dots, and use bound SQL parameters. Do not match `notuniuit.com` or `uniuit.com.example`.

Apply the same exclusion to totals, daily clients, paths, countries, devices, time series, agents, kinds, and referrers, across every public filter including All. Owner exclusions run first. Return the number of excluded observations for the same time/path/agent/kind/group selection as `referralPolicy.excludedViews`, alongside the policy version. Show that count even when every matching observation is excluded. All means all retained public groups after exclusions; those groups still sum to All.

The all-time date boundary starts at the earliest non-owner observation, including referral exclusions. Otherwise the disclosed excluded count could include observations before the displayed start date. Time-series counts still exclude them; an early excluded observation can leave a zero-valued bucket.

This is a **suspected-abuse reporting policy**, not a new bot identity or proof that every matching visit is automated. A legitimate click from an excluded domain is also omitted. The original row, hostname, signature result, reader kind, and reason remain intact. Neither a signed request nor a consumer ASN overrides the referral policy. Private queries can reproduce the pre-policy count.

### Keep collection and history intact

Evaluate the rules at query time for both existing and future observations. No migration, row deletion, reader-kind rewrite, new tracking field, or new dependency is needed. The checked-in policy archive and release's Git revision identify the rules and evaluator; correcting the policy can restore matching observations to reports. Saved research snapshots keep their original method and totals. Record the actual activation time/version separately at deployment. [ADR-0016.6](0016.6-versioned-referral-policy-and-matomo-source.md) distinguishes replay over retained data from reproducing an old published report.

New HTTP(S) and `android-app` referrer input is parsed with the URL parser, normalized, and bounded to 253 hostname characters. Retain Android app identifiers as unreviewed values; the existing report includes such a source. Unsupported schemes, malformed, and oversized inputs yield no referrer. Existing stored unreviewed values remain private and aggregate into the other count; do not manufacture historical normalization beyond the retained hostname.

### Review new cases without rewarding them

Use a private, read-only aggregate report to identify unreviewed hosts, their time distribution, paths, classifications, and repeat patterns. Do not visit a suspicious destination just to establish origin; website content cannot authenticate a request. Capture the evidence, decide between display approval, suspected-abuse exclusion, or no change, add adversarial fixtures, then version and release the catalogue. Under ADR-0016.6, a reviewed pinned Matomo snapshot supplies default exclusions, with explicit local exceptions. Community membership remains a reporting signal, not an authenticated verdict or request-time network dependency.

## Limits and alternatives

This closes arbitrary-hostname promotion in the public referral panel/API and removes the reviewed cluster from public totals. It does not prevent clients sending false headers, eliminate bots that omit referrers or impersonate an approved host, or prove human readership. Unknown-host grouping deliberately preserves those observations while limiting public exposure.

A domain denylist alone loses to domain rotation. Hiding only the offending label leaves all other metrics inflated. Reclassifying every unfamiliar domain as a bot suppresses genuine new discovery. Blocking page access using a spoofable referrer harms legitimate access and does not stop clients omitting the header. Resource exhaustion and rate limiting require separate evidence and are outside this analytics repair.

## Acceptance and release

Use real SQLite against the production query builder to test exact/subdomain matches, lookalikes, case/trailing-dot variants, owner exclusion, historical rows, every public group, scoped queries, all-excluded windows, and unchanged raw evidence. Test thousands of arbitrary hostnames collapse to one generic count and none leak through JSON. Check that reviewed + other + absent referrers reconcile to included views, and included + excluded reconcile to the original eligible population. Confirm the public HTML explains exclusions and the empty state shows their count.

Run analytics tests, affected Worker tests, package typechecks, and the blog build. Deploy the reviewed code/assets together, respecting concurrent article edits; do not accidentally publish or revert another task's work. Verify the live 30-day endpoint removes the offending name, exposes the active version and exclusion count, and preserves reviewed sources. Compare counts within a fixed window when traffic changes during release. Roll back by reverting the policy/code release; there is no database rollback.

## References

- [RFC 9110 §10.1.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-10.1.3): the Referer field describes the user agent's referring resource; it does not provide cryptographic proof of a click.
- [Matomo, Stopping Referrer Spam](https://matomo.org/blog/2015/05/stopping-referrer-spam/): repeated requests with fabricated referral URLs, promotion through reports, and report exclusions.
- [Matomo's community referrer-spam list](https://github.com/matomo-org/referrer-spam-list): prior art for a maintained domain catalogue, not evidence that every listed domain or the initial local rule has been independently authenticated.
- [Plausible ingestion at the reviewed revision](https://github.com/plausible/analytics/blob/543b30185c104ce17900d03c95d95429180acc0b/lib/plausible/ingestion/event.ex#L58-L60): spam-referrer exclusion as a separate ingestion decision. Our implementation retains observations and excludes at query time instead.
