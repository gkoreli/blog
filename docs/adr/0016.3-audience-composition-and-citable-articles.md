# ADR-0016.3: Audience Composition and Citable Articles

## Status

Proposed — 2026-09-03. Extends ADR-0016 and ADR-0016.1, and redirects ADR-0016.2. Research is in progress under `FLDR-0007` (`TASK-0101`, `TASK-0105`, `TASK-0106`); the decisions below are direction, and each engineering step still needs its own evidence before it ships. This document exists so the author's tenets are recorded once, in his words, before they get lost in implementation.

## Author tenets

Goga, 2026-09-03, verbatim from the session (full text in `PROMPT 0011` and `PROMPT 0012`):

> i even want to know the headless browsers on home connections, like Meshclaws or Hermess cloud agents using playwright or cypress or any type of automation, we don't want to miscount or block them, on the contrary, I want to embrace them, anyone can read my articles... I just want full visibility and transparency and categorization, like we need to explicitly know who is who (of course with respecting PII), but lots of people might stop reading articles directly and might use their AI agents like Meshclaws and Hermes agents running on the cloud to read my articles and so on, and I want to know who is reading what, like I am trying to understand how people are using my articles...

> Ideally I would love to have some kind of official citation for my articles as well, like you know how Arxiv or research papers have citations? I want to embrace that as well, like people reading and finding my content valuable and recommending them to cite them as needed, and have some kind of visibility into those citations. Like another example is how scholar.google.com shows Total citations Cited by 51, something like that would love to.

> [on the arXiv BibTeX block] it seems so official and feels like would be quite respectful and increase the credibility or authoritativeness of the articles... But maybe we need some kinda novel way to do it, some kinda agentic native way for citations.

Earlier the same night, on the analytics themselves:

> I don't want to gaslight ourselves, like something is not adding up

> i want the analytics to be honest and believable

> saying browser-like is a little misleading and screams low confidence, like what is the browser like, do we know deterministically or what?

> you need to migrate properly and maintain the historical views, even if it was miscalculated doesn't matter, its okay, we can trace the commit history and know with full honesty what happened and why

Distilled into tenets the engineering must obey:

1. **Composition, not exclusion.** The purpose of classification is to see who reads and how, not to filter "invalid traffic". Automation that reads the articles is audience. Nothing is blocked because of how it reads.
2. **Labels are facts, never confidence.** A public class name states something the request carried: a browser navigation from a residential network, a browser on a cloud host, an HTTP client with a browser User-Agent, an AI assistant fetching for a person, a crawler indexing. No "likely", no "-like", no "suspect".
3. **Know who is who, without knowing who.** Identify agents and services by what they declare (User-Agent rules, signatures, published keys and IP ranges) and by request facts. Never by person. The privacy contract of ADR-0016 stands: no raw IP, no raw User-Agent, no cookies, daily pseudonymous client only.
4. **Never lose history.** Method changes are dated boundaries in the public series, never deletions or silent recomputations. The repository history plus the ADR thread is the audit trail.
5. **Honest and believable beats flattering.** An independent check (a script-based counter, a citation index, a verified link) outranks an internal count. Where no independent check exists, say so on the page.
6. **Embrace agent readers and agent citations.** People will increasingly read through agents. The site should make itself easy for an agent to read correctly and to cite correctly, and should count that reading and those citations as the audience they are.
7. **Credibility comes from persistent identifiers and verification, not from formatting.** A BibTeX block is a handle; what makes it respectable is the identifier behind it and the index that can count references to it.

## Decision direction

### A. Audience composition replaces the Browsers versus Browser-like split

ADR-0016.2's evidence columns stay. The public partition changes from "browser or not" to a set of reader kinds, each defined by request facts, each with one stored reason code per row (the GoatCounter `isbot.Result` model, see research artifact `04`). Candidate kinds, names to be settled in `TASK-0105` with citable vocabulary from device-detector's bot categories and Cloudflare's verified-bot categories:

| Kind | Defining facts |
|---|---|
| Browsers | browser User-Agent, navigation-shaped request, network not on the hosting list; or legacy beacon row; or pre-evidence edge row (disclosed) |
| Cloud browsers | browser User-Agent, navigation-shaped request, network on the hosting list. Most likely an agent's browser. Counted as audience. |
| HTTP clients | browser User-Agent without the request headers every current browser sends |
| Headless browsers | self-declared (HeadlessChrome, Playwright, and similar in the User-Agent) |
| AI assistants | on-demand fetchers acting for a person (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User and similar, per vendor documentation) |
| AI crawlers | training and search-index crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, and similar) |
| Search crawlers | Googlebot, Bingbot, and similar |
| Previews and feeds | link-preview fetchers, feed readers, monitors |

Header absence is a verdict when the User-Agent claims an engine that ships Fetch Metadata on every request: Chromium 76 and later including Android WebView (2019), Firefox 90 (2021), Safari and iOS WebKit 16.4 including WKWebView (2023). caniuse puts support at 95.72% of global usage, Android WebView has sent the headers since Chromium 76, and a 2025 production log analysis in mdn/browser-compat-data #27928 found `Sec-Fetch-Mode` from Safari desktop, iOS, and iOS WebView in the millions. The open-source "weight, not verdict" hedge came from projects without data, and the WebView caveat came from one vendor post without data (artifact 09). So: a claimed modern engine with no `Sec-Fetch-Mode` on a document request is `http-client`; an older or unreadable engine claim without the headers is `legacy-browser`, neither automation nor a confirmed reader. `Sec-Fetch-User` is never required because Safari never sent it. Hosting network is checked before request shape, because the raw Workers Logs (three-day retention, full headers; 844 page GETs over 72 hours to 2026-09-03) showed the bulk inflation is navigation-shaped traffic from hosting networks (430 rows, 374 from one Google Cloud client) that passes every header check, while header absence outside hosting networks was 49 rows and all 12 referred visits carried Fetch Metadata (artifact 09, site measurement). `TASK-0101` closed on prior art plus that measurement; the calibration in `TASK-0104` remains the check on the whole partition.

Public labels, decided 2026-09-03 with Goga ("we need better semantic naming"). The stats page shows four pills that group the reader kinds by what the client was doing: **Browsers** (`browser`), **AI agents** (`signed-agent`, `ai-assistant`: software fetching a page right now on a person's behalf), **Crawlers** (`search-crawler`, `ai-search`, `ai-crawler`, `preview-or-feed`: fetching to index, train, or preview for later), **Automation** (`cloud-browser`, `headless-browser`, `http-client`, `other-bot`, `legacy-browser`), and **All**. The groups are disjoint and sum to All. Below the chart a composition table lists every kind with its reasons in plain words: Browsers, Signed agents, AI assistants, AI search, AI crawlers, Search engines, Link previews, Cloud browsers, Headless browsers, HTTP clients, Other bots, Old browsers. The internal codes stay as they are in the database; only the public wording changed (`preview-or-feed` reads Link previews, and the former `unchecked` kind is gone). The API accepts `traffic`, `path`, `agent`, and `kind`, and rejects combinations whose kind is outside the chosen group.

History keeps its provenance. Rows written before the evidence columns existed are not "unchecked": beacon rows (before 2026-08-26) ran the site's script in a page, which is stronger browser evidence than any header, and carry reason `beacon-script-ran`; edge rows from 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-03 carry the User-Agent verdict with reason `user-agent-only`. For 2026-08-27 onward, migration 0007 reconstructs the network from Cloudflare zone analytics (eight-day retention, client IP served on the Free zone, resolved to ASN through Team Cymru; 1,429 of 1,962 rows unambiguous) and stores it with `asn_source = 'zone-sample'`, so the hosting-network verdict applies to history too. 714 browser-class rows in that window were on hosting networks, 346 of them on 2026-08-31 alone. Header evidence for that window cannot be reconstructed and stays NULL. Source and method: artifact 09.

### B. Agents identify themselves; the site verifies and names them

Investigate Web Bot Auth (HTTP Message Signatures for automated traffic) so that agents which sign requests are named exactly on the stats page. Investigate vendor-published IP ranges for the named AI fetchers. Where an agent declares itself and the declaration verifies, show the name. Where it does not, show the request facts and nothing more.

### C. Agent-native reading (replaces the earlier "citable articles" framing as the first step)

Grounded in artifacts 06, 07, and 08. Cloudflare's own Agent Readiness scan of this zone (2026-09-03) is the working checklist; two of its five Level 1 items are open, and both are free to implement in the Worker.

1. **Markdown content negotiation.** When a request carries `Accept: text/markdown` with higher preference than HTML, serve the post's existing Markdown with `Content-Type: text/markdown; charset=utf-8` and `Vary: Accept`. Cloudflare sells this as "Markdown for Agents" on paid plans; OpenClaw's fetch tool already sends `Accept: text/markdown, text/html;q=0.9`. Record the negotiated representation on the observation row so "read as Markdown" is a visible reader fact.
2. **Typed links.** Add `Link` headers and `<link>` elements: `rel="alternate" type="text/markdown"` to the Markdown endpoint, `rel="describedby"` to the JSON-LD or posts.json entry, `rel="author"`, `rel="license"`, and `rel="cite-as"` (RFC 8574) once a persistent identifier exists. This satisfies Cloudflare's Level 2 "Link Headers" check and the FAIR Signposting profile with no new dependency.
3. **Content Signals.** Add `Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-input=yes` to robots.txt. Search and AI input follow tenet 6 directly. `ai-train` is deliberately omitted until the license question below is settled, because the signal must not contradict the license. Attribution is asked for in prose beside the line; no vocabulary has an attribution-request token.
4. **llms.txt stays but is demoted.** It is inert for AI search (97% of files received no requests in May 2026) and useful only to developer tools. Keep it current at build time; do not treat it as the doorway.
5. **Not for this site:** WebMCP (for interactive tools; a static blog has nothing to register), MCP site discovery (no merged standard), Pay Per Crawl (closed beta, not on Free), NLWeb.

### D. Agents identify themselves; the site verifies and names them

Web Bot Auth is the one shipping standard. Implement verification in the Worker: read `Signature-Agent`, fetch and cache that host's `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`, verify the RFC 9421 signature with `crypto.subtle` Ed25519, require `tag="web-bot-auth"`, key the lookup on the (URL, key) pair. Store the verified agent host on the observation row (`signature_agent`, NULL when absent or unverified). Show it on the stats page as a named reader kind. OpenAI's agent and Google-Agent sign today; Anthropic and Perplexity do not; on-device agent browsers are invisible by design and are counted as what they present, ordinary browsers.

For unsigned but vendor-documented fetchers, match the User-Agent token and, where the vendor publishes IP ranges, verify against them before showing the vendor name; otherwise show the token as a claim. Cloudflare's AI Crawl Control categories (AI Assistant, AI Crawler, AI Search, Search Engine Crawler) and Known Agents' types are the citable vocabulary for the public labels.

### E. Citation: what exists, what does not, and what this site will say

There is no protocol by which an agent tells a source it was cited, no draft of one, and no AI vendor notifies sources. Search Console's generative-AI reports (impressions only, free, worldwide since 2026-08-31), Bing's AI Performance preview, Cloudflare Radar's crawl-to-refer ratios, and Cloudflare's own coming "AEO" panel (mention rate, citation rate, prominence) are the measurement surfaces. None of them is a citation index.

Decisions:

1. **Per-post citation representation, agent-readable.** Each post exposes its citation in the Markdown endpoint and as CSL-JSON (the format DOI resolvers speak) via `Accept: application/vnd.citationstyles.csl+json`, with BibTeX as a secondary rendering for people. Author, title, date, canonical URL, and the persistent identifier when one exists. This is the agent-native half: an agent that read the article can ask the URL how to cite it.
2. **License: decided. Content stays CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.** Goga, 2026-09-03: "the blog itself the code is open source, but the content is mine ... what if i truly want to write a book or become a presenter at conferences? I think i like this approach better: You keep exclusive commercial rights." Consequences accepted: no Rogue Scholar route (it requires CC BY); the training signal stays omitted so robots.txt does not contradict the license; translations and adaptations require permission. Two clarifications to add without changing the license: code snippets inside posts are MIT like the rest of the code, and the license page says quoting and citing with attribution is always welcome. Ownership note: copyright covers Goga's own authorship; exposed essays are his by rule, collaborative engineering pieces to the extent of his prompts, edits, selection, and arrangement, which the transparency pages document. No persistent identifier is planned. Goga, same night: "I can definitely create a long lasting archive if i wanted to"; durability is his to handle, and no evidence exists that a DOI changes citation by AI systems. Withdrawn: the earlier CC BY default in this ADR.
3. **"Referenced by", never "Cited by."** Per post, compose from verifiable sources already in hand or free: AI-assistant fetches (ChatGPT-User and peers) and AI referrals (chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com) from D1; Hacker News search by URL; Bing inbound links; Search Console AI impressions when exported; OpenAlex counts once a DOI exists. Label each source. Reserve "cited" for a citation index.
4. **The open design, for the article, not for code yet.** Because nothing notifies a source, a signed agent could: a Web Bot Auth signer that cites a page could POST a signed notice to a per-site endpoint, and the site could verify it exactly as it verifies fetches. This is a proposal, not a standard, and belongs in the article as the unresolved tension the tenets point at.

### Ordered build list (proposal for Goga)

1. Reader kinds with one reason code per row, plus Web Bot Auth verification and `signature_agent` (sections A and D). This is the taxonomy release and replaces Browsers versus Browser-like.
2. Markdown negotiation, typed links, Content-Signal line (section C). Cloudflare's checklist goes from 3 of 5 to 5 of 5 on Level 1 and gains Level 2's links.
3. Per-post citation representation and the "Referenced by" panel from D1 and Hacker News (section E, items 1 and 3).
4. No persistent identifier (E.2, decided).
5. Calibration: Search Console generative-AI reports and Bing AI Performance as external references beside Cloudflare Web Analytics.

## Non-goals

- Blocking, challenging, or rate-limiting any reader because of how it reads.
- Any per-person identification, fingerprinting, or cross-day identity.
- Claiming that a fetch is a read, that a citation is agreement, or that a count is quality.
- Buying or importing third-party bot lists or datacenter lists wholesale; every list entry stays hand-verified.
